# Nightshift operating brief

Use this brief inside accepted Nightshift delivery or an explicit revise request. Handover is the only entry creating a delivery run. Ready selection requests handover after investigation, and confirmation of that scope releases the request without another handover prompt. Ordinary chat agreement authorizes direct work, including in self-hosting projects, and does not activate delivery. Standalone revise requests use durable review contexts without delivery closing or continuation. Plain review and review-loop requests use the user's direct-agent routines.

## Priorities and authority

Reliability comes first: autonomy and trust as equal parts, never one bought at the other's expense, with trust earned through independent quality assurance. Where both cannot be had, keep what can be trusted, make the blocker explicit and continue independent work. Speed, efficiency and economy count only where they cost neither. All of this stays within the user's authority and limits. Before a destructive, irreversible or outward-facing action that recorded authority does not cover, ask the user; with no user available, when the user is away, block the task that needs it with a `user-decision` blocker and continue independent work. Continue until the authorized work and its required evidence are complete, the user stops it, a resource limit is reached, or every remaining item is blocked without an authorized recovery. A routine failure calls for recovery or a changed approach. It is not an occasion to declare the session done.

Investigate the actual problem before editing. Small work needs a short understanding readback and confirmation; substantial work needs a concise spec of consequential commitments, boundaries and acceptance evidence. Either form ends with a plain question asking whether to begin the work, and only a yes to that question is implementation agreement. Agreeing with an idea, a wording or a direction while it is still being discussed is not, so the session stays interactive and no implementation starts until that question has been asked and answered. Start independent spec assessment through a nonblocking dispatch, then promptly present that same complete stable draft for the user's review while assessment runs. Preserve the worker handle and draft identity. Do not wait for assessment results or work its findings before making the draft available to the user; a progress notice alone is not the presentation. If nonblocking dispatch is unavailable, disclose the sequencing limitation and obtain both reviews without claiming overlap. Implement once the commitments are agreed and the spec assessment is resolved. A governing spec states its commitments only: record the user's acceptance with the runtime `spec-accepted` operation, never in the spec's text, keep assessment counts and the narration of decisions out of it, and write a decision that changes commitments into the commitments themselves through the user-decision route the runtime reference describes. Compatible corrections preserve agreement; material changes need the user's decision. A shared agreement may cover a finite queue. Handover is requested explicitly through the skill or in prose, or by an informed Ready selection. Scope settlement remains interactive; confirmation releases the pending request. Every item the user intends to hand over has its readback or governing decisions settled and confirmed in that interactive phase first, so a queue is handed over once and then proceeds through delivery. A handover requested before that settlement is answered with the outstanding readback or decisions, not accepted; the settlement then happens in the same interactive exchange, and the preserved handover request is accepted once settlement is confirmed. Saved files, backlog readiness and a stage label do not themselves confer authority.

Implement directly with a strong model. Written implementation plans are exceptional guidance for deliberately selected weaker or cheaper implementers. There is no routine plan stage, plan-review ladder or Superpowers dependency.

Nightshift establishes root `.nightshift` as the home for its new project-owned artifacts, whether or not the directory exists yet. Create new standalone governing specs in `.nightshift/specs`; keep feature and backlog documents in their established `.nightshift` locations. This is the workflow's established convention, so the generic per-host fallback does not apply to these new artifacts. Honor user-supplied or explicitly selected document locations and required host configuration paths; applying Nightshift review does not transfer ownership of those files.

## Durable execution

Read [the runtime interface](runtime/REFERENCE.md) before starting or resuming delivery or standalone revision. Accepted handover creates the durable finite queue through `handover`; Ready confirmation releases its already requested handover. Preserve a UUID `handoverId` across acceptance retries. Ordinary chat creates no run, and explicit standalone revise uses `open-review` with its own immutable UUID context. Never replace unfinished work or infer ownership from an open chat. Capture outcome, actual authority, dependencies, publication authority, limits, native identity and intended branch/checkout. Delivery has no attended/unattended mode. Always attempt its host continuation, record observed health or a technical failure and its follow-up, then acknowledge acceptance and say the user can leave before engineering. Failure of continuation preserves authority and closing duties and permits useful work in the current turn; ownership, resource integrity, operation permission and required assurance retain their own gates. Review, skeptic and dialogue dispatches over the actual change need no campaign budget; requested token/cost limits apply to live verification unless the user says otherwise. Record consequential transitions transactionally and reconcile stale or failed writes before dependent work. Read focused status at implementation, review, disposition, completion and compaction boundaries.

Before canonical engineering in every new or resumed controller turn of a delivery run, reconcile status and obtain a successful `claim-controller` result; successful creation through `handover` or `resume` also grants that turn's claim. A status read, native activation or old claim is not a grant. If the claim fails, preserve files and use permitted diagnostics until ownership or admission is resolved. A standalone review context takes no controller claim and refuses one: reconcile its status and actual activity, then use its owner-authenticated operations, whose canonical actions recheck its repair scope and writer ownership, as [the runtime interface](runtime/REFERENCE.md#standalone-review-contexts) describes. To take over another session's run, use explicit adoption authority and the runtime's inactivity and compatibility checks, then reconcile terminated workers while stopped and resume only under authority to continue. Never replace an unfinished run or treat an open original chat as proof of active work. The runtime reference defines the distinct stopped and abandoned-running cases.

Attempt and observe the available host mechanism: a native persistent goal on Codex, the registered functioning Stop hook on Claude Code. Goal states active, absent, blocked, paused, complete and limited are distinct. A user pause/stop or exhausted limit is authoritative, while a technical absence or failed inspection is recorded with a follow-up and useful work continues. Do not replace an unfinished or unrelated goal, infer health from configuration, or use a worker as controller continuation. Neither mechanism relaunches an exited host or Windows. Refresh observations at resumption, adoption, compaction, renewed handover and before a dependent yield. Native recovery requires actual supported authority and observed results; unavailable recovery is disclosed without revoking accepted handover.

Registered hooks restore obligations through exact retained resources. Follow [the resource interface](releases/REFERENCE.md) for preparation, binding and native observations. Ready and Exploring retain their own read-only admission. New runtime work independently verifies native identity, ownership, plugin enablement, permissions and resource integrity; unavailable hook configuration, trust or activation is recorded as integration failure rather than making continuation mandatory for execution. Disabled Nightshift itself, removed registration, invalid resources and denied operation permissions remain refusals. Never enable disabled hooks, grant trust or use development mode to bypass those protections. Observe actual continuation at the required boundaries. A failed or unknown mechanism is recorded with a pending follow-up; recovery preserves that historical obligation and does not ask for handover again. Existing runs keep their exact runtime and original admission rules.

After durable acceptance and its recorded continuation outcome, emit a distinct acknowledgement paragraph as ordinary visible assistant text before engineering. Begin `Handover accepted:`, state the confirmed scope and applicable limits, then say `You can leave.`. If continuation failed, include `Automatic continuation is unavailable`, explain that the failure was recorded and that resumption is needed if the session stops. On Claude, use normal assistant text rather than intended narration in thinking. Keep the tool-use turn active and call runtime `status` after the text, before engineering. Its owner-only ephemeral `acceptanceCheckpoint` distinguishes awaiting a recorded outcome, missing text, available matching evidence and unavailable history. Follow its nextStep rather than assuming narration was emitted: record/reconcile the outcome, emit text then inspect, request engineering, or recover history with bounded diagnostics. A separate paragraph is not an instruction to end the user turn; final-only acknowledgement is permitted only with verified continuation. A tool receipt, future promise, quoted example or later report is not emission evidence. Read-only inspection changes no duty or reminder and grants no permission; actual native output is captured atomically with the dependent transition. Honor a requested hold. Repeat actual text safely on recovery without repeating acceptance. Material agreement changes and adoption renew the binding; compatible corrections and bookkeeping do not.

After compaction, reconcile authoritative state, actual files, native continuation and surviving activity before dependent operations. Preserve other writers and reject stale evidence. Recover routine tool, provider and worker failures within user authority and limits; change approach after repeated failures. Continue independent work when a material decision or capability blocks one item. Await managed dispatches through runtime `wait` rather than yielding while workers remain active. A functioning Stop hook protects accepted delivery while authorized work, required closing or worker reconciliation is actionable. A verified native goal carries Codex continuation; a Stop hook does not replace its ability to begin another turn. An explicit hold suspends engineering and continuation pressure while preserving commitments; safely reconcile workers and resume only with explicit authority. Completion, holds/stops, applicable limits, or waiting solely on user decisions without active or uncertain workers end continuation eligibility. Observations, claims, continuation retries and equivalent proof do not reset no-progress protection. The central store derives semantic progress and preserves cumulative achievement credits across evidence removal/restoration; only admitted execution authority, lifecycle progress or new engineering/assurance achievements permit a fresh reminder sequence. Missing or damaged accounting stops automatic pressure conservatively while preserving independently admitted work. An unavailable mechanism supplies no automatic continuation: disclose that consequence and continue in the active turn. Host exit/reboot relaunch remains outside this delivery.

## Model roles and ownership

Fable and Astra are interchangeable strong-role reference models. Either supported Windows host must be able to carry the complete lifecycle with fresh independent contexts. Choose assignments using task fit, observed strengths, availability, context and cost under the priorities. Establish the problem first regardless of model. There is no fixed controller/implementer team.

Working hypotheses from the user's reports: Fable tends toward deeper deliberation, broad contextual reading, sustained focus and strong visual design; Astra tends toward fast precise coding, computer use, quantitative work and skeptical evaluation of plausible prose, often through effective intuition. Both handle browser interaction well. Use these observations as preferences to test against actual task evidence. Longer reasoning is not itself a correctness signal, and either strong model can perform any required role.

Prefer an equivalent-strength lead reviewer on the other host when suitable and available. The controller chooses placement based on independence from the reasoning that shaped the solution and the actual task constraints. Fresh qualified same-host review is valid. Explicit model requirements bind; a preference permits an adequately capable allowed substitute, with the reason recorded. If the strong reviewer becomes unavailable, secure a permitted strong substitute, preferably on the other host, before applying further repairs to the affected work. Without one, pause those repairs while independent authorized work continues. Advisory assessment never supplies the required strong review coverage.

Use bounded helpers when authorized and useful. Record assignment, host/session, model/effort, and exclusive write ownership. For recoverable in-host work, arrange the registered helper's terminal report and preserve its actual native event evidence as the runtime reference specifies; a turn ending does not establish termination of a continuing assignment or its surviving work. Reviewers, skeptics and reviewer peers never change reviewed inputs. After adoption, exclude every former controller as well as the current controller from new independent assignments. Preserve valid imported historical evidence with its original attribution; an adopter-authored unimported receipt needs fresh independent assessment. Other historical receipts remain usable only when their original attribution, independence and current inputs pass the runtime checks. Peers clone their lead's model and effort. Checks that mutate inputs need isolated fixtures. A lead can ask the controller to dispatch peers and incorporate their assessments; the lead owns credible integrated coverage. A lower-tier supervisor can coordinate assignments, progress and routine recovery; consequential engineering judgment, authority, finding dispositions and final acceptance stay with the controller. Direct or sequential coordination remains valid when it meets the workload without sacrificing assurance or causing avoidable stalls.

## Review and repair

Use [revise-code](../skills/revise-code/SKILL.md) for implementation assessment and repair within the run. Give the fresh strong lead the agreed requirements, complete cumulative change or whole spec, surrounding context, applicable conventions, every dimension below and the recorded acknowledgements: settled dispositions, refuted findings and intentional choices that the governing requirement or the user made, each scoped narrowly and never the author's reasoning. A fresh lead receives no other earlier findings. Do not choose only relevant dimensions or supply the author's correctness rationale. One reviewer is normally enough for a small change. The lead decides whether greater depth or peer assistance is needed for credible coverage. A failed or partial assessment is incomplete even if it reports no findings.

Every finding receives fresh skeptic validation against concrete evidence. A skeptic can validate a small batch; an empty finding set needs no skeptic. Convergence is a reason to investigate, not proof. Keep factual validity, authority and practical value distinct. Every confirmed verdict carries the skeptic's repair proposal: the approach a repair would take, with enough reasoning to judge it. Decide implement, defer with a reason and durable route, skip as an accepted tradeoff, or refuted based on evidence. Missing evidence stays unresolved. A required commitment cannot be waived because its repair is expensive. When a finding's consequence, value or repair quality stays unclear or disputed after validation, relay a dialogue between the reviewer that raised it and the skeptic that validated it through resumed dispatches, or replacements when a session cannot be resumed; never connect them directly, and keep every disposition yourself. Before implementing a finding whose current verdict carries no repair proposal, as when you implement against a refutation, obtain one through a dialogue turn with the skeptic.

When a read-only assessor needs execution evidence, inspect the exact command and fixture source in its untrusted probe proposal, establish that all effects fit authorized verification, and run it through the private-copy probe operation. This uses controller privileges, so the private directory and inventory drift check are not a security sandbox. Return the raw command and output for independent interpretation. Keep execution-dependent claims unresolved until the evidence decides them; do not grant canonical write access to solve a tool limitation.

Validate and dispose the complete intended batch against current evidence, and read back that the runtime recorded each disposition, before editing. Never issue a repair edit in the same message as the validations or dispositions it waits on: the edit uses none of their output, so it looks independent, but calls issued together can run in any order. Interleaved edits can invalidate evidence for remaining dispositions; re-establish that evidence rather than narrowing its scope automatically. A paused strong review gate also pauses further repair application to the affected work until a permitted strong reviewer is available to assess it; advisory feedback does not lift that pause or count as the required review coverage. Recover the strong assignment under the model-role rules above while continuing independent authorized work. After every repair batch, however small, run relevant verification, then resume the reviewer that raised each repaired finding. It records closure of its own repaired findings and reassesses its kind's full cumulative change across all dimensions, including sibling paths and previous repairs; revised specs receive whole-spec assessment. A reviewer that cannot be resumed gets a replacement given its findings. An assessment of any other review kind whose reviewed files the repairs changed, including the latest docs review of the affected tasks whenever a repair changes code, has nothing to close, so do not resume its reviewer: status lists it under `freshTargets` with the task to dispatch a fresh assessment on, an open task whose assessment covers a completed one where possible, so the completed task is not reopened. Dispatch those fresh assessments once the repaired kind's own cycle has settled, with no finding of that kind awaiting repair or closure and its fresh assessment passed, since a further repair would make them stale again. A resumed reviewer verifies repairs well but can develop tunnel vision, so a continued assessment never passes a gate: repair first, and once no critical or important finding awaits repair and none is pending closure, follow it with a fresh strong lead of its kind. A completed fresh assessment with no edits needed can pass after no-edit dispositions resolve its findings; a literal extra LGTM is unnecessary, and its minor findings may be deferred. Repairing any of its findings resumes that reviewer and repeats the cycle. Weak or narrow coverage cannot pass the gate.

Three successive passes raising related findings after repairs are a signal to investigate the shared cause and the repair approach. Same area alone is weak evidence. Missed siblings, repeated invariant violations and repairs undoing earlier repairs are stronger signals. Reconsider the approach or seek a specialist when warranted; do not automatically launch a swarm, halt or ask the user.

### Spec dimensions

- Intent, scope and acceptance: desired outcome, meaningful behavior, boundaries and success evidence.
- Soundness and integration: feasibility in the real project and operating environment.
- Failure, safety and recovery: consequential failures, trust boundaries, data preservation and recovery.
- Clarity, consistency and proportionality: understandable commitments and reasoning without contradictions, missing decisions or unnecessary prescription.

Review mechanisms the spec actually commits to. Do not invent an implementation and report its unchosen details as spec gaps.

### Code dimensions

- Requirements and user experience: agreed behavior, usable flows and relevant accessibility.
- Correctness and integration: normal, boundary, concurrent and failure cases, callers, state transitions and siblings.
- Security and data safety: trust boundaries, permissions, confidentiality, integrity and recovery.
- Design and maintainability: readable local code and coherent system decomposition, ownership, reuse and coupling.
- Performance and resources: consequential latency, throughput, resource use and operating cost.
- Tests and evidence: meaningful assertions and realistic execution, with remaining uncertainty explicit.

### Documentation dimensions

- Claim accuracy: each changed or affected claim matches the code, records and evidence it describes, at the version it names.
- Sweep completeness: documentation, changed or not, that the change left stale, contradictory or missing.
- Backlog conventions: the project's backlog grammar, dependency, history and retirement conventions.
- Sibling consistency: agreement with sibling entries, index excerpts, their records and history files.
- Proportionality: depth proportionate to importance, useful reasoning preserved, nothing overstated or stated as current before it exists.

A docs review judges what the documentation says about the code, with the code as context. Files agents load as operating instructions, such as skills, this brief and the runtime references, are code and need code assessment even when a documentation pass edits them.

One problem is one finding even if several lenses expose it. Every lens receives consideration, without finding quotas, equal-depth narration or duplicated criteria.

## Close and report

Use [revise-docs](../skills/revise-docs/SKILL.md) to reconcile documentation and backlog records with actual delivered behavior and evidence. Preserve source references and rationale for retirement; retired proposals are not shipped features or fixed bugs. Run the real ready parser after backlog changes and record it as the ready parser check: a runtime check that runs the bound release's `skills/ready/ready.js` with `--check`, which exits nonzero unless the parse is clean, with no structural error, notice or missing index. Without the flag the parser exits 0 despite such problems, so a passing check without it establishes nothing about validity. Retain its dependency walk and history conventions. Make these edits after the task advances to documentation: an edit after a review import makes that review stale and blocks the advance. Then obtain a strong independent docs review of the complete change with the documentation dimensions, fresh skeptic validation of its findings and, after every repair batch, the resumed docs reviewer's closure and reassessment followed by a fresh docs review, as under Review and repair; code and docs tasks cannot complete without it, unless a purely mechanical change records its exemption. Edits confined to documentation a docs review alone covers, meaning the backlog (the four indexes, their history files and records under `.nightshift/features`, `.nightshift/bugs` and `.nightshift/patterns`), reports under `.nightshift/reports` and Markdown files at the project root other than the host instruction files `AGENTS.md`, `AGENTS.override.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and `CLAUDE.local.md`, are covered by that docs review. Any other edit to work under code assessment, meaning a code task or a docs task that imported a code assessment, and any edit to operating instructions, also needs a current cumulative code assessment before task completion; descriptive prose in a docs task without one needs only its docs review. The runtime tells these apart by path alone, so when one of those documentation paths holds operating instructions, such as a root Markdown file the project's instructions direct agents to follow, obtain the cumulative code assessment anyway.

Complete task documentation before releasing dependent engineering. Internal governing-spec assessment ends when its independent assessment is accepted. Use [revise-lore](../skills/revise-lore/SKILL.md) to perform one session retrospective after actionable work settles and before the closing follow-up triage; do not repeat those closing steps for each queue item or internal prerequisite. When the only remaining work is a task blocked on a user decision, ask first and close after the answer, since an answered decision invalidates closing evidence recorded before it; closing stays due if the user does not return, and the question carries the same stand-alone context as a morning-report item. Optional follow-up decisions are different: they never delay closing, the morning report or a completed delivery. Examine user corrections, recurring failures, ineffective rules and useful lessons. Prefer concise principles grounded in observed evidence. Propose worthwhile instruction changes as concrete diffs with motivation and independent assessment before asking the user to approve. Do not modify instructions merely because a retrospective suggested an improvement. With no worthwhile proposal, finish the retrospective without manufacturing one.

Every accepted delivery owes a morning report. After the retrospective, save it under `.nightshift/runs/reports/` and record `report` before triage; runtime completion requires it. Write for a reader who saw none of the run: delivered behavior and verification with explicit limits; which required workflows actually ran and any omitted, blocked or recovered obligation; commits and publication status; material retrospective outcomes and reviewed instruction proposals or why none was worthwhile; continuation failures and recovery with their actual effect; and every unresolved item with context, impact and pending choice. A saved acceptance artifact, retrospective label or commit confirmation is not the user report. Do not claim that an unavailable mechanism guaranteed automatic continuation.

The report and the follow-up triage are one hand-off. With the user absent, record the report, record triage evidence stating which decisions stay deferred, complete with those follow-ups pending, and end with the full report itself as the final message. A summary of it, or a statement that it was saved, is not the report, and a run that closes blocked ends the same way, with the blocker in the report's unresolved items. The report as presented always ends with its unresolved items, which list every pending follow-up, followed by a plain question asking whether the user is ready to triage them, or with a plain statement that no decision is pending. When the user is next present in the owning session, through a reply or the SessionStart morning-report notice, present the report that way if they have not seen it, re-presenting it from the saved file yourself instead of sending the user to find it. Their first reply is the delivery authority: record `report-delivered`, then start triage with the first pending follow-up. That reply answers no follow-up; if it says the user is not ready, the follow-ups stay pending until the user asks to triage them. An unanswered final message is not delivery. Until that reply is recorded, the runtime refuses a new handover in the project; any controller session then presents the saved report and records the user's reply before accepting new delivery. When the notice marks the saved report as not current, rewrite it and record `report` again before presenting. Delivery blockers are reported as blockers, never as optional follow-ups.

A standalone review context has no delivery report or closing stage. Its final message lists actual assurance, pending follow-ups and other loose ends with enough context to act on. Historical runs retain the reporting duties their own runtime recorded; they are not silently converted into new handovers.

Triage decision-requiring follow-ups one at a time, with concrete context, a recommendation and the effect of each choice. Every triage item, including the first after a morning report, is a host question: present it through the host's native multiple-choice question tool, such as AskUserQuestion on Claude Code, with the item's full context, the recommendation and the effect of each option inside the question and option descriptions, since text sent alongside the dialog may not render; fall back to a plain-text question only where the host offers no such tool. Use fix now, track or skip where appropriate; preserve the original approve, adjust or revert terms for deferred approvals. Wait for the answer before presenting the next item. When the user is unavailable, preserve unanswered items in the run and report that they remain decisions, not completed work. Mid-run ideas go into this follow-up record so they do not derail the authorized queue.

Apply the tracking edits that triage decisions call for after triage is recorded, keeping them to the backlog and, where a tracking edit needs it, such as a link to an archived entry, the other documentation a docs review alone covers. Rerun checks whose inputs they touched, such as the ready parser check, and obtain a closing docs review of them through the runtime's closing record before completing the run; its findings take the same skeptic, disposition and repair loop and never reopen tasks. Before completing a task or the run, rerun under its own name every check that status lists in `staleChecks`, which names each latest check of any task, completed ones included, and of the closing record that no longer passes on current inputs. A change outside that documentation is not tracking: on a running run it needs ordinary cumulative reassessment, and on a completed run a finding that needs one is deferred with a durable route, or stays unresolved when it is a required obligation. For a handed-over run that completed before its follow-ups were answered, record the closing checks and closing docs review as bookkeeping after the tracking edits. The run's recorded limits still bind them; when a limit prevents them, report the tracking edits as not independently reviewed rather than overriding it. An unresolved closing record, or tracking edits a limit kept from its review, are not independent review coverage for publication, so publishing those edits needs another independent review.

Commit coherent local work under the user's Git policy and required hooks. Keep scratch files, probes and raw evidence in the project's ignored `.tmp` and never stage them. When the run's scratch status says `.tmp` is not ignored, tell the user which rule to add (`/.tmp/` in `.gitignore` or `.git/info/exclude`), in the morning report when they are away, rather than editing their ignore policy, and carry its other notices, such as commits to squash before publishing, into the report's unresolved items. After every commit, by the controller or a helper, confirm that it landed on the run's intended branch and checkout; on a mismatch, stop the dependent work and reconcile it only within recorded authority, since a reconciliation that would rewrite history or discard work needs the user's decision as Priorities and authority describes. Publication proceeds only when authorized and independently reviewed, including any applicable advance handover authority. Do not repeat settled approvals. Without publication authority, finish locally and report unpublished status. Report delivered work, verification and its limits, accepted tradeoffs, deferred work and every incomplete obligation accurately. Identify the required workflows actually performed and any omitted or blocked stage from their execution evidence. Reading a skill, recording a stage label, or substituting a direct review or ordinary file edit does not establish completion of that workflow. When required machinery is unavailable, preserve the evidence and capability limitation; do not claim the lifecycle complete. Later recovery records when the missing work actually happened without claiming it had happened earlier.

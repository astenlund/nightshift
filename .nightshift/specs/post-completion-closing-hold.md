# The post-completion closing hold

## Outcome and authority

A completed delivery whose closing record still owes post-completion closing work holds the project's next handover back. Today the hold is hard to see and, without the owning session, cannot be cleared: Ready lists a complete record only while it is the current selection and its morning report awaits its recipient, `handover` refuses a held delivery with `overlapping-run` or `delivery-consistency-unavailable`, depending on which of the gate's checks the held work trips, without naming the record; status does not say that the record blocks the next handover, and for holds such as a failed or uncovered closing check it reads the closing review's next step as null; and `close-manifest` answers `close-not-needed` for every complete record. This design makes the hold visible wherever a session meets it and gives the user an explicit route to drop the held work through the administrative close.

It discharges the bug "A post-completion closing hold blocks the next handover invisibly and cannot be closed", shaped by the user on 2026-11-20:
- **Route.** A session other than the owner clears the hold through the administrative `close`, on the user's authority, rather than by adopting the completed record. A held record closes as a stopped record does, so a live owner does not refuse it; the owner keeps its own routes until then.
- **Evidence failures.** A complete delivery whose completion cannot be authenticated, as distinct from one that owes closing work, can also be dropped through the administrative close, with the failure named in what the user approves. Close admits a record only while every evidence body its saved state references can still be read; a record whose evidence bodies cannot all be read stays refused, and that lockout is tracked separately.
- **Counting.** A record closed from `complete` counts as closed, not as complete, as [Close settled records](close-settled-records.md) already rules for every closed record.
- **Governance.** A short spec settles the commitments before implementation.
- **Trigger.** Giving the ready parser a route as a recorded check, the most common way into the hold, stays with the quick win "Make the ready parser usable as a delivery check".

What the closing record owes after completion, as the creation-gate paragraph of [the runtime reference](../../internal/runtime/REFERENCE.md) states it, is unchanged. Only the hold's visibility, its refusal code and the close route change.

## Commitments

### 1. One predicate

For every complete delivery that is not retired, one shared predicate gives exactly one of four outcomes:
- **Discharged.** Its completion is discharged and its closing record holds nothing back. Other obligations, such as a morning report awaiting its recipient, keep their own gates.
- **Held.** Its completion is not discharged, and every reason is outstanding closing work recorded since completion.
- **Unauthenticated.** Its completion is not discharged, and at least one reason is a failure of evidence rather than work. Closing work found beside it is named too.
- **Unavailable.** The judgment of this record cannot be made: the accounting and provenance verification the creation gate requires before it reads a close row does not hold, because its accounting is not current or cannot be read, or its independent provenance, bindings or discharge-index entries do not verify, which close refuses with `close-accounting-unavailable` and the remedy close already gives; or the record meets one of its own bounds (its completion row, its own accounting and completion-evidence allowances, or the selected delivery's reserved input). It is never read as discharged or as not held.

A store missing a table its own format requires (`runs`, `active` and `history`, and `artifacts` from version 2 on), or lacking either of `accounting_commits` and `discharge_index` while it holds any accounting evidence (a record's saved accounting, or any row in the surviving table), is damaged as a whole: no production writer leaves that state. Every surface refuses the store with its existing store-level refusal and gives none of its records an outcome. A store that has every table its format requires and no accounting evidence is not refused as a whole: its records are judged as today, each unretired legacy record that is not discharged joining the grouped legacy refusal, compacted or not.

Once its accounting is established, the predicate judges from the evidence the discharge judgment then reads: the completion row and its provenance, and the snapshots and closing baseline that judgment resolves. An invalid or unreadable body in that set is a failure of evidence. Evidence the judgment does not read, such as a stored check output, changes no outcome, so a delivery otherwise discharged hands over as today; an operation that reads every body the record references, such as close's write, an exact replay or a status or owner read, still meets it as today.

Running out of an input allowance shared across a pass of several records is not an outcome of any record: the records left unjudged are neither unavailable nor discharged. Each surface reports it at pass level: the creation gate keeps today's shared-allowance refusal, with its kept verdicts and retry, and Ready shows a store-level notice beside the store's records saying that some complete deliveries could not be judged, as commitment 4 describes.

Outstanding closing work is: the latest invocation of a named closing check recorded since completion whose files the latest closing assessment has not seen, as the creation-gate paragraph requires; a latest closing check that failed or is pending; an unsettled closing finding or a closing finding repaired after the latest closing review; a pending closure or carried finding that belongs to the closing record; a latest closing review that is incomplete, unattributed or missing a docs dimension; an active or uncertain worker, since completion admits none, so every such worker was started by closing work after it; and a task reopened since completion, which `block` can do on a complete record, together with every completion condition the reopening leaves unmet, such as the reopened task's docs review or the docs exemption `block` clears, and the spec readiness of a code task whose linked governing spec task was reopened. This list is complete. Every other reason a completion is not discharged is a failure of evidence, such as an invalid or unresolvable snapshot, an invalid closing baseline, or a closing record replaced since completion, which no production writer does while the status stays `complete`.

Two kinds of record get no outcome: a retired complete delivery, which Ready omits and close answers `close-not-needed` as today, and which status newly identifies as retired instead of reporting an outcome; and a complete standalone review context, which keeps today's behavior on every surface. A nonselected delivery carrying a current settled-record verdict is not judged afresh and has the Discharged outcome on every surface, as the creation gate already accepts it. A verdict stands in only for judging discharge: evidence a verdicted record loses after its verdict changes no surface's outcome until the record is next judged in full, as the runtime reference states, while an operation that reads every referenced body, such as status's ordinary read, still meets the loss as commitment 3 says.

The creation gate, status, Ready, `close` and `close-manifest` judge a complete delivery through this one predicate, so none of them can disagree about it, as the creation gate and `close-manifest` disagree today. The predicate reports each cause by the check, finding, closure, review, worker, task, evidence or accounting it concerns.

### 2. The creation gate

A refused handover names each blocking record the pass judged before it refused, by its ID with its outcome, or for any other blocker its status, and says that status, which reaches any record by its ID, and `close-manifest` give its causes and routes, and that Ready lists its outcome and causes. It does not state causes or routes itself. Its code is that of the highest blocker present, in today's order: the grouped refusal for records without independent accounting; then `delivery-consistency-unavailable` for an unauthenticated or unavailable complete delivery; then `report-delivery-pending`, which as today applies only to a discharged, unretired selection, so a held selection whose report awaits its recipient refuses with its hold rather than its report, and Ready's `present-report` pickup for it is unaffected; then `overlapping-run` for every held delivery, selected or not and whichever closing work holds it. No blocker this spec does not concern changes its code, such as an undischarged nonselected stopped delivery keeping `delivery-consistency-unavailable` and the selected unfinished delivery or an active worker keeping `overlapping-run`. A held delivery is named whichever code the refusal carries. The only change to an exact replay of the selected delivery's own acceptance is that the selected delivery being held no longer refuses it, as an uncovered closing check or a pending report does not refuse it today; it then returns the delivery unchanged, with no new revision or history row, while the creation gate's settled-verdict upkeep during the pass stays as the runtime reference states it. Every refusal that reaches a replay today still reaches it: those for other records, a nonselected held delivery included under its new code, the grouped and shared-allowance refusals, the selected delivery's own unauthenticated or unavailable outcome, and a failure of the ordinary read that returns the saved state. A pending report alone never refuses an exact replay and does not rank its code: a replay refused by another blocker carries the highest code among the blockers that refuse it, while the refusal still names the pending report.

### 3. Status

On every complete delivery commitment 1 gives an outcome, status reports its outcome, causes and the routes that apply to it now wherever its ordinary read of the record succeeds, and no closing way forward reads `null` while the delivery is held or unauthenticated. Where that read fails on an unreadable referenced body, including one the predicate does not read, such as a stored check output, status fails as today, and commitment 6's failed-read rule governs the owner's report. An unavailable outcome is reported with its cause; status still returns. With freshness verification skipped, the outcome reads `'reconcile at acceptance'`, as `staleChecks` does. Status still writes nothing.

### 4. Ready

Ready's run state reports every complete delivery whose outcome is not discharged as an open record carrying its outcome and causes, pointing to status and `close-manifest` for its routes. It decides the two offers independently: `present-report` by the existing rule of [Ready's run state](ready-run-state.md), which limits it to the current selection, and `close` from the delivery's outcome and the one check Ready keeps for a complete delivery, the adoption-eligibility verdict on its saved workers that it shares with close: a held or unauthenticated delivery gets the close offer unless that verdict refuses. The saved-marker screen of the running and stopped offer does not apply to a complete delivery, since the predicate has already established its accounting and an accounting failure reaches Ready only as the unavailable outcome. An unavailable delivery gets `{offer: 'none', reason, message}` with its cause, as a refusal shows for every other record. Ready reads no evidence body for its offer; readability, like every other condition commitment 5 leaves to close, is judged by `close-manifest` and `close`. Nothing the hold scan yields, whether an outcome or a pass-level shortfall, removes a record that rule reports or changes its `present-report` pickup. A complete delivery that rule does not cover, such as a nonselected one, a current selection whose report was delivered or one without a handover, gets no pickup and shows only its close value: never adopt, `present-report` or `report-unrecorded`, since adoption of a complete record is out of scope and the other two claim a report is owed. The interface owns how no pickup is represented. The close offer is decided this way for every complete delivery the scan judged; a reported record that is discharged, or that a shortfall left unjudged, keeps today's close value. When the scan runs out of its shared allowance, the outcomes it established before the shortfall are kept, and the store gets a notice beside its records saying that some complete deliveries could not be judged; the notice does not mark the store unreadable, and it alone brings up the Run state section, whether or not the store has anything else to show. Ready's existing bound on current states, reported as `run-overview-bound`, is unchanged.

This amends Ready's run state, whose module never reads `history` or accounting tables and does not evaluate discharge, and whose per-store `unavailable` entry contributes no records. The tradeoff, accepted here: Ready reads what the predicate needs for each complete delivery that is not retired and has no current settled-record verdict, within the creation gate's own input bounds, in a read-only transaction that writes no verdict.

### 5. Routes

**Owner discharge.** Runtime mutations, report confirmation included, reach only the current selection, and an owner on this release can only hold the current selection; read-only status reaches any record. The owner discharges a held delivery as today: it reruns each closing check until it passes, settles the closing findings and closures, reconciles its workers and obtains the fresh complete closing docs review the creation-gate paragraph requires. The current selection's owner has this route while the delivery is held, unless a task was reopened since completion, since `unblock` is refused on a complete record. Status names the work the causes need, and the route also says that the run's limits (deadline and dispatch allowance) and the need for a working Git checkout to dispatch an assessment may refuse some of that work. Whether they will is not inferred: the owner's own actions decide it. Every owner action also reads the record through the ordinary path, so an unreadable referenced evidence body removes the owner route too. Where no owner route remains, the administrative close is the only route the runtime offers, and it applies only while the bullets below admit the delivery. Where the delivery's own close eligibility also refuses, such as an adoption-eligibility code like `worker-activity-unknown`, `close-accounting-unavailable` or the unavailable outcome's cause, no route is available until that refusal is resolved: status and `close-manifest` report its code and the remedy close gives for it, Ready does so for the worker refusals its shared verdict finds, and none of them offers close there. Every other close condition stays on the surface Close settled records assigns it, as for every other record: `close-manifest` reports the older-release guard, which Ready and status do not judge, and only close's own admission and write enforce the project-operation condition, the write-ownership lock and refusals bound to the request. When the refusal is an unreadable referenced evidence body, the delivery has no route in this release: status fails as commitment 3 says, `close-manifest` and `close` name the tracked bug "A modern record with unreadable evidence has no administrative route" beside close's refusal, and Ready, which reads no body, decides its offer as commitment 4 says: unreadability alone neither withholds an offer nor restores one the worker verdict refuses. An unauthenticated or unavailable delivery has no owner route; its recovery is the one the runtime reference already names.

Only status computes routes, and only `close-manifest` and `close` judge close eligibility. The creation gate and Ready report outcomes and point to them.

**Administrative close.** Any session, on the user's authority, drops a held or unauthenticated delivery through `close`, whether or not it is the current selection:
- `close-manifest` and `close` agree on each complete delivery's eligibility: they admit a held or unauthenticated one while the accounting and provenance verification the creation gate requires before reading a close row holds and every evidence body its saved state references can still be read, since the close write saves through the ordinary path that reads them all. Close never commits a closed delivery the creation gate could not count as discharged, every condition the gate applies to the closed record included, its read bounds among them: for the selection the reserved current state and latest row, which the closure itself enlarges. When the closed record would not fit, close refuses with a named cause and writes nothing, and the delivery keeps whatever route it had; how close computes this stays with close. `close-manifest` judges the current record. The creation gate's judgment of the closed record, which only close's write can make, can still refuse an approved manifest with `close-bound-unavailable` or `close-accounting-unavailable`, writing nothing. When the creation gate's pass runs out of its shared input allowance, close refuses with `close-bound-unavailable` whatever it judged before: it cannot show that the next handover counts the closed record. Where retried handovers keep no verdicts, no route in this release makes room: handover and close stay refused until the charged input fits the allowance or verdicts accumulate. That lockout is tracked separately. When the close row is a record's latest row, the creation gate judges its discharge from the latest-row read it has already charged, and for the selection reserved, rather than charging that row again. Both hold for a stopped record's close as well. One with an unreadable evidence body, and an unavailable one, are refused by both with their cause; a discharged one answers `close-not-needed`. How close checks this stays with close.
- Otherwise the delivery closes exactly as a stopped record does under [Close settled records](close-settled-records.md): the same manifest, reply, report choice (`waived` while a handed-over record's report awaits its recipient), older-release guard, accounting condition and worker eligibility, with no owner liveness check, so an owner still live afterwards meets `run-closed`.
- The manifest names the causes the close drops, and for an unauthenticated delivery says that its evidence could not be authenticated. Those causes are part of what the user's approval binds, and a member already closed under the request's approval is projected from what that approval recorded, so the later store transaction of the same close and an identical retry reproduce the approved manifest without judging a record that no longer has an outcome.
- A closure from `complete` records `previousStatus: complete`, which widens the value set Close settled records gives that field, and the creation gate counts the record discharged by its close row, as it counts every closed record.

`hold` and `stop` are not routes: they would turn a completed delivery into a stopped one.

### 6. The owner's final report

When an owner session ends its work, including follow-up triage and post-completion tracking edits, the rule keys on the outcome status reports after the session's last runtime action that could change it. When that status reports its delivery Held, Unauthenticated or Unavailable, its final message says that the delivery will block the next handover in this project and names its outcome, its causes and the routes that apply to it now, as status reports them. When status cannot be read, or that current outcome cannot otherwise be obtained, the final message says so and that the delivery may block the next handover, presenting it neither as discharged nor as a confirmed blocker. Otherwise, whether the delivery is discharged, closed, retired or without an outcome, this rule adds no blocker statement. It never presents a blocker only as a residual limitation. The operating brief states this rule.

## Lifecycle and consumers

The predicate is computed at every reading from saved state and the project files historical discharge reads, for every complete delivery commitment 1 judges afresh, and is never persisted. Closing a held or unauthenticated delivery is a new transition from `complete` to `closed`. The implementation finds every consumer that reads a record's status or a closure's previous status and confirms each treats a record closed from `complete` as closed; `tools/fortnight.js` already counts closed runs on their own and needs no change. It likewise finds every consumer that presents the new outcomes, refusals, offers and the shortfall notice, including the ready and handover skills.

Every statement of these claims changes with this release: which record statuses `close`, `close-manifest` or a direct request to close admit; that `close-not-needed` answers every complete delivery; that Ready never reads history or accounting or does not evaluate discharge; what `overlapping-run` names; and what a close manifest and its approval contain and bind and what an already-closed member is projected from in a later store transaction or an identical retry; and which selection `report-delivery-pending` applies to, a held selection awaiting its report refusing with its hold. It also covers every statement of what lets the creation gate count a closed record discharged within its input bounds. This includes the Manifest section, the approval field of the status model, the Eligibility section with its accounting bullet, the Creation gate paragraph, the consumer table and the already-closed member rule of Close settled records, and the store-reading, open-record, pickup, Output and presentation rules of Ready's run state, the Output contract included for what Ready's run state holds and what brings up its Run state section. The sweep is by claim over `internal`, `skills`, `hooks`, the specs and the backlog: a hit is in scope when its whole paragraph states one of these claims, whatever its wording. Its search terms are `close-not-needed`, "nothing is left to close", "complete record", "completed record", "stopped record", "close a stopped", "whose owner has ended", "`running` or `stopped`", `overlapping-run`, "cannot be adopted", "never reads", "does not evaluate", `previousRevision`, "projected", "projection", "exactly these keys", `close-manifest`, "could not count as discharged", "close row", "pickup", `runState` and `report-delivery-pending`.

**Which release runs each surface.** `close` and `close-manifest` run through the administrative migration entry, which uses the current release from any session, subject to close's older-release guard, which still asks a session bound below the first release that ships close to close from a new session. The creation gate, Ready, status and the operating brief run the release the reading session is bound to, so commitments 2, 3, 4 and 6 reach sessions bound to this release or a later one. A session bound to an earlier release keeps that release's behavior, which preparation already flags with `older-release-bound`. The release notes say that such a session, a resumed owner included, starts a new session on this release to see the outcome and its routes. The implementation also confirms that an earlier release's creation gate reads a record closed from `complete` as discharged; if not, the release notes say so as well. The implementation follows the rule-identity rule of the settled-record verdict paragraph of the runtime reference and re-pins the verdict tripwire if a pinned module changes.

## Related tracked work

- **Hold, stop and block on a complete record.** The quick win "Decide whether hold, stop and block are admitted on a complete record" decides whether those operations should be refused there. Until it does, this spec counts a task reopened since completion as held work and names neither `hold` nor `stop` as a route.
- **Shared-allowance lockout.** [Cut store v4 and reduce the release system](../features/store-v4-cut.md#carried-fixes) carries the fix for the lockout the administrative close paragraph accepts, folded there on 2026-11-27 from the bug that tracked it.
- **Unreadable evidence bodies.** The bug "A modern record with unreadable evidence has no administrative route" tracks the lockout this spec leaves: close refuses a held or unauthenticated delivery while an evidence body its saved state references cannot be read, and retirement does not take a modern record, so such a delivery, once it blocks handover under commitments 1 and 2, has no administrative route.

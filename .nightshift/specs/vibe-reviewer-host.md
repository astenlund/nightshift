# Vibe reviewer host

## Outcome and boundary

Let the runtime dispatch lead assessments through Mistral Vibe on GLM, so that lead review tokens, the largest share of review spend, come out of the Vibe plan instead of Claude or Codex usage. The fortnightly baseline in [the assessment worklist](../reports/assessment-worklist-20261018.md) measured, over this store's repair batches, about 367M tokens on fresh leads, 165M on resumed leads and 49M on skeptics. It realizes the feature recorded in the history index [Admit Mistral Vibe and GLM as a reviewer host](../FEATURES_HISTORY.md), on the evidence of [the GLM 5.3 review evaluation](../reports/glm-5-3-review-evaluation-20261023.md) and [the cross-paired run](../reports/seeded-defect-baseline-20261024.md#cross-paired-glm-53-run).

GLM is admitted as a strong lead for every lead kind, including a fresh assessment that closes a task or record, and is never admitted as a skeptic. The evaluation found GLM at parity on detection, decoy refutation and clean-change false positives, but its lead raised more weak scope findings, and as a skeptic it confirmed every unmatched finding it judged, from its own lead and from Opus's alike. The skeptic is the role that filters noise, so it stays on the Claude and Codex strong families. Detection was at the ceiling for every model in the suite, so GLM's ability to close a task on harder defects is unmeasured, an accepted risk of this design.

In scope: the `code`, `spec` and `docs` assessment kinds, fresh and replacement dispatches, with a GLM lineage's closures and lead dialogue turns carried by replacement since Vibe sessions cannot be resumed here (see Resume), in delivery runs and standalone review contexts alike. A GLM lead therefore takes over fresh-lead spend and pays for each closure at replacement cost; the resumed-lead share stays with Claude and Codex lineages. Out of scope, each staying as today: Vibe as a controller host; Vibe for in-host workers registered through the `worker` action, peers included; the `skeptic` kind, including a skeptic's dialogue turn; implementers; model families other than GLM on Vibe; operating systems other than Windows.

## Admission

`internal/runtime/models.js` replaces the single strong test with admission by role. A role is `skeptic` for the `skeptic` kind and `lead` otherwise: the `code`, `spec` and `docs` kinds and an omitted kind, which dispatch already records as `code`, so validation and import key the role on the same defaulted kind the receipt carries. The Claude and Codex families and their floors are unchanged and admitted for both roles. Vibe gains one family, `glm`, with floor 5.3, admitted for `lead` only. Vibe's plain identifier form is `glm-<major>[-<minor>]`, mirroring the sibling forms: version parts of at most three digits with no leading zero, and a missing minor compared as zero, as `compareVersions` already does. It matches the identifier Vibe journals as its active model (`glm-5-3`); a variant or suffix is refused like any other non-plain form. A Vibe candidate may also name the alias `glm`, which `validateRequest` resolves, before every other candidate rule, to the newest GLM identifier in the launcher's table of model entries (see Launcher); the candidate then carries that exact identifier through admission, dispatch, attribution and the receipt, so no receipt ever records the alias. A `requiredModel` of `glm` resolves the same way and is then compared with the resolved candidates. Admission of an identifier later than `glm-5-3` is subject to the launcher having an entry for it: an admitted identifier the table lacks makes the candidate ineligible (see Eligibility before reservation), and a release that adds its entry makes it runnable.

Both existing gate points apply the role: `validateRequest` in `internal/runtime/review.js` checks every candidate against the role of the request's kind, and `readReceipt` checks the receipt's host and model against the role of the receipt's kind. A Vibe candidate on a skeptic request is refused with `review-strength`, whose message names the role. The receipt's `strength` stays `strong`, meaning admitted as strong for its kind, so the fresh-assessment test in `internal/runtime/lifecycle.js`, which requires `strength === 'strong'`, is unchanged and a clean fresh GLM assessment passes the gate. `requiredModel` and `substitutionReason` keep their rules; a candidate list may mix Vibe and other hosts.

## Launcher

`internal/runtime/hosts.js` gains `runVibe`, promoted from `tools/seeded-defects/vibe-launcher.js`, and `executable` and `runAgent` accept `vibe`. The suite then uses the runtime's launcher for a Vibe stage whose model the runtime admits, such as a GLM lead, and keeps its evaluated-host injection for every stage whose model the runtime does not admit, such as a GLM skeptic or either stage of `mistral-large-4`. The facts below about installed Vibe 3.0.0 come from an isolation probe of 2026-10-25, live headless runs and a reading of Vibe's source, whose results the release's acceptance report records.

- Process: the native `vibe.exe` resolved by `resolveTrustedExecutable`, run headless in the dispatch's private copy (`--workdir`, `--agent nightshift-reviewer`, `--prompt-file`, `--output json`, `--output-dir` under the attempt's artifacts, `--time-limit` derived from the attempt's timeout, `--enabled-tools read_file`, and never `--trust` or `--add-dir`) inside the runtime's Windows job, with stdout and stderr bounded below the job's output capacity, as the suite's launcher does. Startup, termination-proof and artifact-write failures follow the same paths as `runClaude`.
- Isolation: like the Claude launcher (`--safe-mode`, `--strict-mcp-config`) and the Codex launcher (`project_doc_max_bytes: 0`, hooks, plugins, apps and multi-agent off), a Vibe reviewer's instructions, tools, tool descriptions, prompts, model and configuration come only from the launcher and Vibe's built-in defaults; every other source is a channel to close. The launcher closes them with four mechanisms, created afresh for every attempt:
  - A launcher-owned Vibe home: `VIBE_HOME` points at a folder under the attempt's artifacts holding only the reviewer agent file `agents/nightshift-reviewer.toml`, the reviewer prompt `prompts/nightshift-reviewer.md` and an empty `trusted_folders.toml`. This closes every user-scope channel Vibe reads from its home: the user's configuration file, `AGENTS.md`, hooks, tools and their description overrides, prompts, agents, skills, plugins and trust grants, as well as the experiment cache. The credential is unaffected, since Vibe reads it from the operating system's keyring or an inherited `MISTRAL_API_KEY`, so nothing is copied under `.nightshift`.
  - An environment built from an allowlist rather than inherited: the system variables a Windows process needs (`PATH`, `SYSTEMROOT`, `WINDIR`, `COMSPEC`, `TEMP`, `TMP`, `LOCALAPPDATA`, `APPDATA`), `VIBE_HOME`, `MISTRAL_API_KEY` when the controller's environment holds it, and `USERPROFILE` and `HOME` pointed at an empty launcher folder, which closes `~/.agents/skills`, the one user-scope channel Vibe locates from the home directory. No `VIBE_*` variable other than the launcher's own `VIBE_HOME`, and none of the unprefixed variables Vibe's settings classes read reach the reviewer.
  - An untrusted run: with no `--trust` and the launcher's empty trust file, the copy is untrusted whatever the user's own trust grants say, so the project scope (`AGENTS.md` files including their lazy injection into `read_file` results, and every `.vibe` and `.agents` folder in the copy) is closed. Headless Vibe runs an untrusted folder without prompting.
  - The reviewer agent file, which outranks the user, project and environment layers and sets: `active_model` to the candidate's identifier with the launcher's own `models` entry for it, taken from the launcher's table of model entries, which maps each identifier it knows to its underlying name, provider, offered thinking levels and the level a requested `low` runs at, and today holds two entries, `glm-5-3` (underlying name `zai-glm-5-3`, provider `mistral`, levels `low`, `high` and `max`, `low` for a requested `low`) and `mistral-large-4` (underlying name `mistral-large-4`, provider `mistral`, levels `off` and `high`, `off` for a requested `low`), with image support turned off in every entry, since Vibe otherwise takes it from its own table of known models; an entry admits nothing, and no family admits `mistral-large-4`, which only the seeded-defect suite's evaluation launcher runs; experiments and telemetry off, MCP, connectors, subagents and background processes off, project context and commit signature off, the model catalog and registry skills off, `enabled_tools = ["read_file"]`, and `system_prompt_id` naming the launcher's prompt. With experiments off, the model exists only through this entry, so no remote experiment can route the identifier elsewhere. The effective-configuration check and the effort mapping take their expected values for the active model from the same table entry. The launcher's prompt replaces Vibe's CLI system prompt; Vibe's native harness core still prepends its own fixed preamble and appends its capability sections, which count as built-in defaults.
  What stays open, by Vibe's design: the always-loaded builtin `vibe` plugin skills, whose `skill` tool is refused and whose bodies Vibe can still load when the prompt mentions one as `/name` outside a code span; those bodies are Vibe's own text and count as built-in defaults, since the user's and the project's skills are closed; the write, edit, shell and skill tools Vibe advertises despite `--enabled-tools`, every call of which headless Vibe refuses as `tool_denied`, with the dispatch's own check that the copy is unchanged behind it; code mode's `run_typescript`, which cannot be removed (see Sandbox execution evidence); and the admin layer, fetched from Mistral's managed-config endpoint whenever a Mistral key resolves, which no per-invocation mechanism can outrank or turn off and which is accepted as part of the provider boundary on condition that the effective-configuration check below passes on every attempt. If the launcher cannot create its Vibe home, prompt, agent file or environment, the attempt fails before launch with `vibe-isolation-unavailable`.
- Effective-configuration check: after every attempt, before its report is used, the launcher compares the effective configuration Vibe records under `config` in `export.json` with an expected configuration, the launcher's own value for each key it set, else Vibe's built-in default. The comparison covers every key except an allowlist of keys that cannot change the reviewer's instructions, tools, agents, prompts, model or endpoint, whatever their value: keys that only affect Vibe's terminal interface, operational keys such as update checks, timeouts, the auto-compact threshold and logging, the redacted credential values, and catalog and provider entries other than the active model's. The active model's entry (underlying name, provider, thinking level and offered levels) and its provider's entry (endpoint and API style) are compared like any other key. The allowlist is fixed by classifying every key of the Vibe 3.0.0 configuration schema against that property, and a user's off-default operational preference passes. A key the 3.0.0 schema does not have, which only a later version can add, has no known default to compare with; it is recorded by name on the attempt and the receipt and not compared, under the accepted risk of newer versions stated below. Any difference, from any layer, active on that day or not, fails the attempt as `unusable-review` naming the differing keys, and the next candidate is tried; an `export.json` whose `config` lacks a compared key fails the same way. Hooks and instruction files are not configuration keys; the Vibe home, environment and untrusted run close them.
- Version: Vibe 3.0.0 is the floor, the version the isolation was probed on, and any later version is accepted automatically. Before launch the launcher reads `vibe --version`, and a version below 3.0.0 or one it cannot read or parse makes the candidate ineligible with `vibe-version-unsupported`; after the attempt it requires the `vibe_version` in `export.json` to equal the version read before launch, failing the attempt as `unusable-review` otherwise. Each Vibe attempt and receipt records the version. Accepting later versions unprobed is an accepted risk of this design: a later version can add an instruction or extension channel that is not a configuration key, which neither the mechanisms above nor the effective-configuration check would see, and new configuration keys go uncompared. Rerunning the isolation probe on a newer version, and raising the floor or tightening the launcher in a release when it finds an open channel, is the maintainer's recourse.
- Not admitted: if the installed-host evidence shows a channel open that these mechanisms do not close, or the Vibe 3.0.0 configuration schema has a key whose effect cannot be classified and the export does not record it, Vibe is not admitted, since a reviewer that obeys instructions in the change under review or in configuration outside the launcher is not independent. Nothing Vibe-specific then ships: no launcher, role admission, import branch or prompt sentence, and none of the guidance or tracking below. The evidence is recorded and the delivery task is blocked as a user decision, so an unattended run records it and continues independent work. The per-host split of `tools/fortnight.js`, which is repository-only and useful without Vibe, may still proceed.
- Eligibility before reservation: the `dispatch` operation in `cli.js` checks a Vibe candidate's eligibility (an identifier the launcher's table of model entries holds, a readable version at or above the floor, read before launch, and the launcher's ability to create its isolation) before `requireDispatchFits` reserves time for the candidates and before the worker is registered. An ineligible Vibe candidate is dropped from the attempts with its reason recorded beside the substitution reason, consumes no unit of `maxDispatches` and no reserved time, and the remaining candidates run; a request whose only candidate is an ineligible Vibe one fails with the eligibility code (`vibe-model-unknown`, `vibe-version-unsupported` or `vibe-isolation-unavailable`) before any worker is reserved. A version that changes between that check and launch fails the attempt as `unusable-review` through the export comparison.
- Tools: `read_file` only, through `--enabled-tools`.
- Prompt and report: Vibe has no structured-output option, so the report schema is stated in the prompt; the report is taken from the last assistant message's last untagged or `json` fenced block, else its outermost braces, and then validated by `validateReport` unchanged.
- Attribution: the session id from `export.json`, and the model from the session journal's runtime state, which must name one active model across every generation, equal to the requested one.
- Tokens: `usage.total_tokens` from `export.json`, which counts the attempt alone. Vibe's count is its own accounting and is not comparable with Claude or Codex figures, which the receipt's host already makes visible.
- Host failure: an attempt that ends without a usable report records as `hostFailure` the error `export.json` reports, else the end of stderr, bounded at the runtime's 2,000-character limit, ending the attempt's error message as for the other hosts. Vibe's JSON output exposes no partial output, so like Claude attempts its attempts are not checked for output loops; the output bound above caps a runaway stream.
- Effort: `glm-5-3` offers the thinking levels `low`, `high` and `max`, and Vibe silently resets a level a model does not offer to `high`; on the wire `low` sends no reasoning and `high` and `max` are the same. Every table entry states its own offered levels and names the offered level that sends no reasoning, which a requested `low` runs at, and an entry is added only when it offers `high`, which every other requested effort runs at; `mistral-large-4` offers only `off` and `high`, so it runs at `off` for a requested `low` and at `high` otherwise. Since `max` is never used, a GLM lead runs at `high` for a requested `medium`, `high`, `xhigh` or omitted effort, whatever effort the controller or implementer uses, and at `low` for a requested `low`. The launcher writes that level into its model entry, and after the attempt reads the effective level back from the active model's entry in `export.json`: a level other than the one it wrote fails the attempt as `unusable-review`. The receipt records the effective level, `low` or `high` for GLM and `off` or `high` for Mistral Large 4. `validateRequest` keeps today's permitted set, so no Vibe candidate is refused for its effort, and the seeded-defect suite's pinned `xhigh` runs GLM leads at `high` without a change.

## Sandbox execution evidence

`run_typescript` stays available, so a Vibe lead can run code in Vibe's sandbox: a copy it transcribed, or a project file it loaded through `read_file`, which in the audited sessions ran as shipped when the module needed no Node built-in. The evaluation found such claims real but sometimes wider than the recorded call, and [the 2026-10-30 audit](../reports/mistral-large-4-review-evaluation-20261030.md#sandbox-execution-claims) found 8 of 52 audited claims that cite execution going beyond their recorded calls and the loaded-file case in 6 of 16 sampled Mistral Large 4 receipts. The runtime neither records nor checks a sandbox run, unlike a probe. The lead prompt for a Vibe attempt, built per attempt in `dispatchReview`, states that sandbox code can load project files, that a sandbox run is still not execution evidence of the project, even of project code it loaded, and that deciding execution goes through a probe request, as for every host. The skeptic, always a Claude or Codex model, validates each finding against concrete evidence as today. Two paths have no skeptic: closures in a replacement GLM lead's report, and a clean fresh GLM assessment with no findings. On those paths the prompt rule is the only guard, and whether GLM follows it is model-owned behavior that the installed-host evidence below observes on each path separately: a fresh lead on a claim that needs execution, and a replacement lead closing a finding whose repair can be decided only by execution. A closure never passes a gate, since a fresh lead must follow it, and the clean-pass case is part of the accepted risk above, so no runtime gate changes.

## Resume

Vibe resume cannot serve Nightshift: a resumed Vibe session keeps the working directory it started in and ignores a new `--workdir`, while every dispatch gets a new private copy and the earlier copy is removed. `resolveContinuation` therefore refuses `review.resume` on a Vibe receipt before anything launches, with a new code `resume-unsupported` whose recovery names `review.replaces`, so no tokens are spent on a resume that cannot work; `closureRoute` in `continuation.js` returns `replace` for a named dispatch whose receipt host is `vibe`, so status's `resumeTargets` advise replacement. Closure and lead dialogue turns of a Vibe lineage go through replacement, whose candidates may be any admitted lead, Vibe included.

## Receipt import

`readReceipt` gains a Vibe branch beside the Claude and Codex ones. The Vibe launcher writes Vibe's JSON output entries as `events.jsonl`, already hashed into `eventHash`; `dispatchReview` also records on a Vibe receipt `attributionEvidence`, the project-relative paths and SHA-256 hashes of the attempt's `export.json` and of every generation's runtime-state file in the attempt's session journal. Import refuses a Vibe receipt with `unattributed-review` when `attributionEvidence` is absent or empty, lacks the attempt's `export.json`, or omits a generation present in the attempt's session journal, and refuses a Claude or Codex receipt that carries the field. The session, outcome and model checks read exactly the listed files and no others: import refuses unless every listed file still has its hash, the listed export's session id equals the receipt's session and its outcome is finished, the listed runtime-state files name exactly the receipt's model, and the report taken from the events by the launcher's rule equals the receipt's findings, probes, coverage, closures and positions, with `changed-result` or `unattributed-review` as for the other hosts. Claude and Codex receipts are imported as today.

## Placement

Placement is controller guidance in the operating brief, not a runtime gate; the runtime admits any admitted candidate order.

- Leads: a GLM lead on Vibe, named by the alias `glm` so that controllers need not track GLM versions, is the default first candidate whenever Vibe is available, to save Claude and Codex usage. The fallback candidates in the same request are ordered Codex before Claude (Sol, then Opus), whichever host the controller runs on, keeping Claude usage for interactive work, where its harness is strongest. When Vibe is unavailable (executable missing, signed out, usage exhausted), the fallback candidates run as today, with the substitution reason recorded.
- Peers: a GLM lead that returns incomplete asking for peer coverage cannot be given peers, since peers clone their lead's model on Claude or Codex. The controller treats that report as incomplete and dispatches a fresh lead from the fallback order, whose peers can clone it.
- Skeptics: each skeptic runs on a different host from the lead whose findings it validates, preferring the controller's own host, so that lead and skeptic sit on different hosts even when only Claude and Codex are available. With a GLM lead, or a Codex lead under a Claude controller, the skeptic runs on the controller's host; only when the lead runs on the controller's own host, as a Sol fallback lead under a Codex controller does, the skeptic moves to the other host. For a Claude or Codex lead, when the host other than the lead's is unavailable, the skeptic falls back to the lead's host in a fresh session, with the substitution reason recorded; the brief's rule that fresh qualified same-host review is valid still covers that fallback. For a GLM lead, the skeptic runs on the controller's host, else on the other of Claude and Codex, never on Vibe; when neither is available the validation waits as a recorded blocker, as any unavailable strong reviewer does. This replaces the other-host preference for skeptics.
- Disposition: GLM leads raise more weak scope findings, which the skeptic tends to confirm as true observations, so the controller's value and obligation decisions carry that filtering.

## Guidance

Placement above is the single lead and skeptic order controllers receive; every guidance site states it or defers to the brief, and none keeps an other-host preference for leads.

# Settled-record verdicts at delivery creation

## Outcome and authority

The delivery creation gate stops re-proving, at every handover, what it already proved about records that have not changed since. Once the gate has judged a record settled, it records a verdict bound to that record's revision and stored bytes, and later gates confirm the verdict from metadata instead of parsing, replaying and re-judging the record. The gate's work then follows what changed since it last ran rather than the project's whole history, so the 64 MiB input allowance bounds the records still in play instead of every record the store has ever held.

This fixes [The creation gate re-reads every settled record at every handover](../BUGS.md#the-creation-gate-re-reads-every-settled-record-at-every-handover), whose stopgap shipped in 3.3.39. The user chose on 2026-11-17 to fix it on the current store and carry the property into [the store v4 cut](../features/store-v4-cut.md), to confirm verdicts from metadata with freeze triggers as retirement does, and to have the gate write verdicts as it judges.

## Commitments

1. **What a verdict vouches for.** The gate writes a verdict for a record it has judged in full when that record is not the selected delivery, is discharged with no open closing work, has no active worker, and is either a modern record with current independent accounting or a retired record whose certificate validated. A verdict binds the record's id, revision, current-state byte length and digest, its latest history revision and that row's byte length, its accounting journal's latest revision, its acceptance handover id, and the identity of the judging rule. Unretired legacy records, records that are not settled and the selected delivery are judged in full at every pass, as today.
2. **Confirming a verdict.** A later gate accepts a verdict, without reading the record's state, history, accounting or completion evidence, only when every bound value still matches the store's metadata (the record's revision and state byte length, its latest history revision and that row's byte length, its journal's latest revision) and the verdict's rule identity is the current one. Any mismatch makes the verdict stale: the gate judges that record in full and replaces or removes its verdict in the same pass. Every legitimate change to a record goes through a save that advances its revision, so a reopened or amended record is re-judged without a special case.
3. **What the allowance bounds.** Records with an accepted verdict are not charged to the shared input allowance, including the up-front check of current states and latest rows, and the allowances, the per-record completion-row bound and every refusal they raise keep applying to the records judged in full. Store-wide checks that read only metadata (record count, identity sizes, selection, orphaned evidence, retirement references) keep running over every record. Acceptance identity stays unique across verdicts and fully judged records.
4. **Freeze protection.** While a record's verdict matches its current revision, the store refuses rewriting that revision's current row, history rows or accounting rows in place, through SQL triggers in the manner of the retirement guards; a save to a new revision is unaffected. The gate checks that this protection is installed exactly as the runtime defines it. If it is missing or changed, the gate accepts no verdict, judges every record in full and reinstalls the protection within its allowance; a store that cannot then fit is refused as today.
5. **Threat model.** Verdicts keep the gate's present protection against rollback, against a hand edit that writes a new revision, and against a same-revision edit while the triggers stand. They do not defend against a deliberate actor with write access to the database who forges a verdict row or alters the triggers and the rows together; neither does the present gate against one who forges consistent accounting, provenance and certificates. A dropped or unreadable verdict only costs a full judgment.
6. **Rule identity.** A release that changes what the gate's judgment of discharge accepts or refuses changes the rule identity, so every verdict written under the old rule is stale and re-judged once. The runtime reference states when the identity must change.
7. **Failure branches.** A verdict that is malformed, names a record the store does not hold, or duplicates another settles nothing: the gate judges the record in full where it exists and removes the offending verdict. A verdict table or trigger whose shape is not the runtime's own is treated as missing protection under commitment 4. A pass that refuses creation writes nothing, as today, so a store already past the allowance before any verdict exists stays refused, with no administrative route to write its verdicts, by the user's choice of 2026-11-17; the stores measured for the 3.3.39 stopgap were each within the allowance, so they gain verdicts on their next delivery-creating handover.
8. **Other writers of the store.** Runs bound to earlier 3.3.x releases keep working on a store that holds verdicts and triggers: none of their writes may be refused by the protection except an in-place rewrite of a verdicted revision, and nothing they do may make the new gate accept a stale verdict. `compact-history` either carries verdicts and protection over exactly or drops them, leaving the next gate to judge in full. Migration inventory keeps its own bound and is not changed by this spec.

9. **The artifact scan stays store-wide.** The run-bound artifact scan, which parses and identity-hashes every artifact carrying a run id at every handover (8,216 artifacts, 23,238,227 bytes in this repository's store on 2026-11-17) and is not charged to the input allowance, keeps covering every record, verdicted or not, by the user's choice of 2026-11-17, since artifacts are not trigger-protected; its growth with history remains, as the bug entry records.

## Acceptance evidence

Deterministic fixtures show: a store whose settled records together exceed the input allowance admits creation once those records hold verdicts, while the first pass that writes them is bounded as today; a stale verdict by each bound value, and by rule identity, leads to a full judgment and a replaced verdict; a same-revision in-place rewrite of a verdicted record is refused by the store; missing or altered protection leads to full judgment; malformed, orphaned and duplicate verdicts settle nothing; a record reopened by a new revision is re-judged; and the writes of the earlier 3.3.x runtime on such a store succeed. Each refusal and fallback fixture asserts its specific reason, and the fixture for the allowance fails against the 3.3.40 gate. Read-only probes that drive the changed gate against copies of field stores report the shared charge on the first and second passes. No installed host has to run the change before release; the changelog says whether one has.

## Out of scope

The v4 store design, which inherits only the property that the gate's work follows what changed; migration inventory's bound; hydration of completion evidence, tracked as [Creation hydrates every completion snapshot to judge a completed run's discharge](../bugs/creation-hydrates-completion-snapshots.md), which this spec makes run once per settled revision rather than at every handover. The existing `discharge_index` table indexes the closing-discharge provenance artifacts the gate reads for each record, not a judgment the gate could trust, so it serves no part of this. Runtime changes ship with a version increase. This spec authorizes no publication.

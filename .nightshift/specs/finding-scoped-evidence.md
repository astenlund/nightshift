# Finding-scoped evidence

## Outcome and boundary

A skeptic's validation, a finding dialogue and a probe result stay usable while the files they are about stay unchanged. An edit elsewhere in the project neither refuses them nor withholds them. To make that possible, a finding names its affected surfaces and a probe names the files its result depends on.

This is the validation and probe part of the conditions step in [the assessment worklist](../reports/assessment-worklist-20261018.md), developing [assessment change conditions](assessment-change-conditions.md). It delivers [Keep every affected surface when merging findings](../features/finding-affected-surfaces.md) and the bug "Probe evidence about the host is discarded on any edit". It also delivers the quick wins "Hand stored probe results over after a line-ending renormalization", "Keep stored probe results when a resumed review adds an artifact", "Give skeptics the probe evidence stored for their finding", "Name the stale check when completion refuses" and "Name the changed inputs of a stale assessment in status".

Lead assessments, closures, checks, the final release gate and the commitment binding of validations and dialogues keep their current rules. Status conditions and the retirement of the `rules` string, spec-edit conditions, the closing baseline and the recorded-decision operation stay with the rest of that step. Comparing a validation with the snapshot of the review that raised its finding is not part of this change.

## Affected surfaces

A lead finding gains a required `surfaces` list naming each place where the alleged problem exists. Each entry has `path`, the file's project-relative path; `location`, the function, section or line within it, or an empty string when the claim concerns the whole file; and `evidence`, what that surface shows. The list may be empty. A lead finding also gains a required `basis` list of the project-relative paths of files the claim reasons from without alleging a problem in them, which may be empty. The report schema stays identical for every lead dispatch, so a resumed session's cached prefix survives. The reviewer prompt asks that surfaces list every place the problem appears, that basis list every other file the claim depends on, and that one problem appearing in several places be one finding listing each place. The finding's `evidence` remains complete on its own.

A surface is identified by its `path` and its `location` with surrounding whitespace removed, and that identity governs lead admission, assignment and verdict matching. Entries of one claim that share an identity are consolidated into one surface with their evidence joined, both before a finding is recorded and when surfaces become a claim's assigned surfaces, so supplied surfaces are treated as recorded ones are and a skeptic's answers are matched over the consolidated identities. An entry is malformed unless it is an object with a nonempty string `path`, a string `location` and a nonempty string `evidence`, and a malformed entry refuses the report before any identity is computed, as the report's sibling lists refuse theirs. A `basis` that is not a list of nonempty strings refuses the report the same way, and a path repeated in it counts once. A well-formed path that does not resolve to a project file is not malformed.

A skeptic's assigned claim takes its surfaces and basis from the recorded finding, whatever the dispatch request supplies, so its answers bind to the record that controls scope and disposition. A dispatch with no recorded finding behind a claim, as the seeded-defect harness makes, uses those it supplies. A skeptic verdict gains a required `surfaces` list with one entry per assigned surface, carrying `path`, `location`, `verdict` and `evidence`, where `verdict` is confirmed, refuted or unverified and judges whether the alleged problem exists at that surface, not whether the observations in its evidence are true. Basis files receive no verdict and owe no repair. A report that omits, repeats or adds a surface is refused with `missing-validation`, and importing a verdict for a recorded finding requires an answer for exactly its recorded surfaces, an empty list when it has none, however the skeptic was assigned, or is refused the same way. A claim assigned no surfaces answers with an empty list and keeps today's finding verdict rules.

When surfaces were assigned, the finding verdict follows them: `unverified` when any surface is unverified, `confirmed` when at least one is confirmed and none is unverified, and `refuted` when every surface is refuted. Any other combination is refused with `invalid-verdict`. An unverified surface therefore leaves its whole finding unresolved under the existing rule that missing evidence is unresolved, until a later skeptic settles it.

Surfaces and basis are recorded with the finding when its review is imported and do not change afterwards. The surface verdicts are recorded with the validation. A surface refuted on a confirmed finding stays named in status and in the closure turn of the reviewer that raised the finding, and a repair is owed only for the confirmed surfaces. The closure turn lists the confirmed surfaces and asks the reviewer to close the finding only when each is repaired. A closure stays one answer per finding, so no per-surface state gates it, and the later fresh assessment remains the assurance behind it. A confirmed finding with no surfaces owes its repair as a whole, as findings do today. The requirement that a consolidated finding lists every surface it covers also applies to a lead merging its peers' findings, without depending on peer dispatch.

## Validation and dialogue scope

A finding's scope is the set of paths its surfaces and basis name, when every one of them is a file in the raising review's context snapshot by exact match. A finding with no surfaces, whatever its basis names, with a path that does not resolve this way, or recorded before this change has no scope. An unresolved path never refuses the report, since that would discard a strong dispatch over a naming slip. When a finding has a scope, it also contains the governing specs the raising review's commitments name, so the requirement text relied on stays bound by rule and not by a model naming it. A task with no governing spec adds nothing, and this never gives a scope to a finding that has none. The scope of a finding raised by a spec review is instead that review's deciding inputs, whatever its surfaces and basis name, and a path outside that set neither enters the scope nor voids it, so its validation and dialogues stay bound to the deciding inputs and implementation edits do not reopen them. In a spec review `basis` names only deciding inputs. A deciding conclusion that needs an input outside the set still returns incomplete naming it, and other project files remain orientation and probe context that a claim may be checked against without being named. Scope is resolved once when the review is imported and recorded with the finding's surfaces and basis, so a finding carried into a replaced closing record keeps it and no later reader needs the raising review.

A skeptic or dialogue receipt keeps its full assignment snapshot, and the private-copy check against its assignment is unchanged. Every comparison of such a receipt's currency compares only the finding's scope, whether at the end of its dispatch, when a receipt is read for import, or at `validate`, `dialogue` or `dispose`. Reading a receipt to run a probe it requested does not use finding scope: that probe's currency follows its own declared files, as the Probes section states, and the receipt's provenance and commitment checks still apply. Each path's recorded identity comes from the receipt's context snapshot, with the existing tolerance for line-ending renormalization. A scope path absent from that snapshot has no recorded identity, so the finding has no scope for that receipt. A refusal names the changed paths and the way forward.

Without a scope, a receipt keeps the comparison its kind has today: the whole inventory, or the deciding inputs for a spec-kind dialogue. An operation concerning several findings, such as the end of a dispatch, compares the union of their scopes and falls back as above when any of them has no scope. An operation naming one finding compares only that finding.

Scope bounds what an edit can invalidate, and it does not certify that the skeptic relied on nothing else. Fresh cumulative assessment and the final release gate remain the assurance over the whole change.

## Probes

A probe request gains a required `paths` list naming every project file whose content its result depends on. An empty list declares a probe about the host or tools alone. The prompt says so. When a path does not resolve to a file in the requesting receipt's context snapshot, the probe keeps today's whole-inventory comparison before launch and today's digest comparison in handoff. A receipt without the field does too.

Declared files govern evidence currency. Before launch, `stale-probe` compares only the declared files against the receipt's recorded identities, so an edit elsewhere leaves a pending probe runnable, and a declared file that changed or is missing refuses it, naming that file. Execution integrity is a separate check that declarations cannot narrow: at launch the runtime captures the project inventory under the receipt's recorded selection, and `probe-input-drift` refuses the result when that inventory changed during execution. `canonicalUnchanged` keeps that meaning, including for a probe that declares nothing, so a concurrent edit during a probe still costs that probe's result.

The private copy holds the project's current files under the receipt's recorded inclusions and exclusions, so a selected ignored file stays present and an excluded file stays absent. A file removed since the receipt is absent from the copy, and that refuses the probe only when the file is declared. A probe result records its declared scope with file identities.

A stored result is eligible for any later assessment of its task, skeptics included, while its declared files are unchanged under the same renormalization tolerance. A result with an empty declared list is always eligible. Its files carry the probe's purpose, command and declared paths, so the receiving reviewer judges whether it still bears on the claim. Among eligible results with the same executable, arguments, fixture files and declared paths, only the newest is attached. One dispatch attaches at most twenty results, newest first, and tells the reviewer the names of the eligible results left out and, for one superseded by a newer identical probe, the result that replaced it. A handed-over result keeps one name across every dispatch of its task, derived from its stored identity and independent of which other results are attached, so neither selection nor the bound moves a citation. A dispatch reports to the controller which stored results it withheld and the declared paths that changed. A result withheld by the digest comparison is reported with that comparison named and null paths, so an empty list never stands for an unestablished cause. A result stored before this change keeps today's digest comparison. A replaced closing record starts without stored results, as it does today.

`repair` no longer clears `probeEvidence`. Results whose declared files changed stay recorded and are withheld by the handoff comparison, so nothing that withholds evidence depends on the clearing. [The runtime reference](../../internal/runtime/REFERENCE.md) states what an edit does to pending and stored probes.

## Refusals and status

Every refusal from the review and docs gates states each failing task with its cause: changed inputs with their paths, changed commitments, a comparison that could not be established, a named failing or missing check, or unmet assurance for any other gate failure. A delivery's `complete` refuses a review-gate failure with `verification-required` when every failing task fails only because of a check, and with `stale-review` otherwise. `advance` and a review context's `complete` keep `review-required`, and docs-gate refusals keep `docs-review-required`. Wherever a task or closing record is refused because a check is failing, pending, stale or missing, whichever gate reached it, the refusal names that record and the check with its cause. Apart from the delivery `complete` rule above, those refusals keep their existing codes, and a closing record's refusal separates its check cause from its other causes. Each `freshTargets` entry carries the same distinction for its stale assessments, with null paths when they cannot be established, and an empty path list never stands for changed commitments or an unestablished comparison.

## Lifecycle

The new fields are additive inside the existing run state, so the store version does not change. A missing field means the no-scope behavior above. A finding with no scope records none. Malformed surfaces, `basis` or `paths` in a saved record count as no scope, as does a recorded scope that is not a nonempty list of nonempty path strings or, for a finding not raised by a spec review, that lacks a path the finding's own surfaces or basis name. Every snapshot a receipt carries must pass its whole-snapshot integrity check before any scoped or declared-file comparison, including for a probe declaring no files. A receipt with a snapshot that fails it is refused, whatever its kind, as a comparison that could not be established, with null paths. A context snapshot absent from an old record is not an integrity failure and takes the no-scope fallback. Every reader of a finding's claim, a validation's snapshot and stored probe evidence handles the new fields, including continuation context and closing-record findings. Interrupted imports stay retryable as they are today.

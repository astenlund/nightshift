# Make resumed reviewers actually cheap

Moved from the quick wins on 2026-10-03 at the user's direction, once its evidence had outgrown a quick win. The text is the quick win's, arranged under headings, with its links adjusted to this location.

## Origin

Found on 2026-10-02 in run `61461833-3c7e-4449-8282-67b3df7dd564` and raised by the user afterwards. [Resumable reviewer and adversarial repair dialogue](resumable-reviewer-dialogue.md) has the reviewer that raised a finding resume to verify its repair, for continuity on brittle repairs, and settles that the resumed reviewer also reassesses the full cumulative change. In that run a resume was not reliably cheaper than a fresh review. The fresh Fable docs review (request `9e79e429`) used 1,671,065 tokens at a reported list cost of $4.99; its resume (`250cbdf8`) used 963,253 of its own at about $4.53, although its receipt in that run recorded the session's cumulative 2,634,318 tokens, the defect [Resumed Claude dispatches record cumulative session usage](../BUGS_HISTORY.md#resumed-claude-dispatches-record-cumulative-session-usage), fixed in 3.3.3. The fresh Astra code assessments used 836,555 tokens (`5f8b21fc`) and 781,819 (`a91e2bf2`), while the resume of the first (`e3e56cc6`) used 1,193,771 of its own. Both resumes started without a cache hit: the first resumed Claude call read nothing from cache and wrote 181,223 tokens, although every cache write in both Claude attempts used the one-hour lifetime, and the first resumed Codex call had none of its 95,007 input tokens cached. So did all six resumes that completed with receipts in the 3.3.0 acceptance campaign under `.tmp/resume-live-edb0199e`, on both hosts, including Codex resumes that started 23, 39 and 72 seconds after the previous turn ended, so cache expiry does not explain it. Two candidate causes, untested until the Claude resume probe below found on 2026-10-03 that the second alone did not prevent a hit on Claude, and the Claude and Codex schema probes confirmed the first on both hosts on 2026-10-20, with Opus 5.5 and Sol, the Codex one also clearing the second there: the structured-output schema the runtime builds for each dispatch (`schemaFor` in `internal/runtime/review.js`) bound, until 3.3.14, the new request id and, on a lead's resume, added the closure or dialogue fields, so it differed between a review and its resume, and on Claude it becomes the `StructuredOutput` tool; and each resume runs in a new private copy, as [the runtime reference](../../internal/runtime/REFERENCE.md#independent-assessment) describes, whose working directory the host records at session start. The reviewers' system prompt files were byte-identical across each review and its resume. The resumed reviewer then also reassesses "its kind's full cumulative change across all dimensions", as [the operating brief](../../internal/workflow.md) requires.

## Direction

Measure a resume's own cost on each host, find why it starts without a cache hit, which the schema probes under Evidence answered on 2026-10-20 for Opus 5.5 and Sol, and make it cheap without losing the continuity it exists for. One option, resuming only to verify closure of the repaired findings while the fresh lead does the full cumulative reassessment, would fall below the user's review-loop routine, which the resumable reviewer spec adopts as its floor and under which each resumed reviewer reassesses its category's full cumulative change, and would put a fresh lead after every repair batch rather than only once no critical or important finding awaits repair, so it needs the user's decision. Relates to [Weigh minor fixes against the review cycle and keep the rest as follow-ups](../QUICK_WINS.md#weigh-minor-fixes-against-the-review-cycle-and-keep-the-rest-as-follow-ups), whose advice may be refined once resumes are cheap; to [Skip resuming a reviewer with nothing to close](../QUICK_WINS_HISTORY.md#skip-resuming-a-reviewer-with-nothing-to-close), delivered in 3.3.5, after which only reviewers owing closures are resumed; to [Dispatch reviewer peers and return their evidence to the lead](reviewer-peer-dispatch.md), which resumes the lead to integrate peer evidence; to the feature [Proportionate review of tracking edits](tracking-review-cost.md), which on 2026-10-03 scoped closing reviews to the tracking edits since triage with the user's agreement, while a resumed reviewer's full reassessment stays as described here; and to the Exploring draft [Orchestration efficiency](orchestration-efficiency.md), which attributes most dispatch cost to context re-read on every call. Runtime and guidance changes ship with a version increase. Tracking does not authorize implementation.

### Lineage-constant schema

Shipped in 3.3.14 on 2026-10-20. `schemaFor` in `internal/runtime/review.js` now builds one schema for every lead dispatch and one for every skeptic dispatch, identical from a reviewer's first call to its last: the request id is a plain string, a skeptic's findings carry no id enum or count bounds, and a lead's `closures` and `positions` are always present, unbounded and required, empty unless the dispatch asks for them. The identities the schema no longer binds, `validateReport` checks when the report arrives, as it already did: the request id, a skeptic's assigned finding set, a continued lead's closures and a dialogue turn's positions. An entry in the closures of a report that is not a continued lead's outside a dialogue turn, or in the positions of one that is not a lead's dialogue reply, is refused with `invalid-result`, a check new in 3.3.14, as is a closures or positions value that is not an array. The prompt now asks for the request id verbatim in requestId and the full finding id verbatim in closures and positions, and says when those are to be filled. The probes above justify the change on Opus 5.5 and Sol; whether a runtime resume on 3.3.14 reads its history from cache is to be read from the native event files of the first resumed dispatches on this release, which their receipts name, and is not claimed until then. Open after 3.3.14, decided at triage on 2026-10-20 to track rather than change yet: a resumed reviewer whose closure names a wrong id is refused, and since its attempt is already marked failed in its session the `missing-closure` refusal is recorded as `resume-failed` with the missing-closure message inside it, so the lineage is routed to a replacement and the saving is lost for that reviewer; the first resumes will show whether it happens, and the fix, treating closure and position content refusals as session-independent, waits for that reading. Wording debt for the next release that touches `review.js` or the runtime reference, tracked at the same triage: the positions refusal message names a dialogue reply though a skeptic's dialogue reply is refused by it too, the reference's refusal clauses are not scoped to a lead's dialogue reply, its skeptic sentence says the schema no longer binds the ids where the present contract is that it does not bind them, the comment above the array check describes the two checks below it, and the reference's sentence that every lead report carries both fields overstates the schema requirement, since `validateReport` accepts a lead report without either field.

### Cached input pricing by host

How cheap a resume is depends on the host, as the user pointed out. Codex bills Astra's cached input at 25 credits per million against 250 for uncached input and 1,250 for output, and its credit billing has no separate cache-write charge ([OpenAI's Codex pricing](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing), read on 2026-10-03), a 90 percent discount. The Claude API reference bundled with Claude Code 2.1.288 (pricing table cached on 2026-09-25) prices cached reads for Fable 5.1 at $0.25 per million against $10 uncached and for Opus 5.5 at $0.20 against $4, a 95 to 97.5 percent discount, with cache writes at about 1.25 times input. Long cached context therefore still costs on Codex: at those base rates, cached reads came to an estimated 372.6 of Astra's 584.8 credits in this trial, about 64 percent. Third-party pages (truefoundry.com and eesel.ai, citing OpenAI's API documentation) also report that a prompt above 272,000 input tokens is billed at twice the input and cache rates; Codex's pricing page does not mention it, and the resumed thread's last three calls held 274,343 to 282,095 input tokens, so whether it applied is unverified.

### Compacting a reviewer before its resume

The user then suggested compacting the reviewer's context so that a resume starts small, in their words: "idea: could we have astra compact before returning? that way, context would be lowered before a potential resume". `codex exec` (Codex CLI 0.158.0) has no compaction option, but the app-server protocol the runtime dispatches Codex through has a `thread/compact/start` request taking only the thread id (`codex app-server generate-json-schema`, read on 2026-10-03), so the runtime could compact a reviewer's thread; compacting a thread directly through the app-server was tried the same day, as the next paragraph records, while doing it from the runtime is untested. Open for measurement: compacting before returning or lazily at the start of a resume, which avoids paying for compactions no resume uses (in this trial, three of seven Astra dispatches were never resumed); whether the compacted summary keeps the findings, evidence locations and completed checks that a resume exists to carry; how much a verification round then saves; and whether Claude, whose cached reads cost far less and whose sessions compact themselves, needs it at all.

A first trial the same day compacted the follow-up batch's Astra reviewer thread through `thread/compact/start` before resuming it for a verification round. The compaction ran as a 92-second turn for which the thread reported only a `last` total of 7,550 tokens with zero input and output and an unchanged thread total, so its own cost is unknown, and it fired the `preCompact` hooks configured for Codex, the user's own and Nightshift's. The resumed round's first call held 40,473 input tokens, 13,184 of them cached, where the thread's last call before compaction held 153,576, and the round used 738,056 input tokens, 89.0 percent cached, and 2,166 output, against 1.6 to 2.9 million input tokens at 97 to 99 percent cached for the uncompacted verification rounds of the earlier loop. The compacted reviewer closed all eight repaired findings and reported nothing new; the other reviewer found one more minor finding. The rounds differ in size, the compacted round's cached share fell to 89.0 percent, which at Codex's rates left about half of its estimated 39.4 credits on uncached input against 50.1 to 100.1 for the uncompacted rounds, and the compaction's own cost is unreported, so whether compacting saved anything overall is not established. The user then refined the timing, in their words: "if this works out, we could suggest running compaction eagerly using a bg process while the controller works on the fixes"; once repairs begin, the reviewer that raised the findings is normally resumed, unless a replacement takes over because the resume fails, so a compaction started then in the background is rarely wasted and its latency overlaps the repairs, while the saving itself stays provisional until measured.

Both trials resumed into a first call of about 40,500 tokens with the same 13,184 cached, which suggests the compacted summary plus a fixed cached prefix; the round totals still depend on how much each round reads.

The user then asked whether compaction should run only above a context threshold, in their words: "would it be a good idea to add a threshold for codex compactions, so we only compact if we exceed, say, 200k tokens?". The recommended starting threshold is 150,000 tokens, set with the user on 2026-10-03 and to be tuned once the compaction's own cost is measured, for example from account-level usage rather than the thread's report. Compaction saves little for a thread not far above the roughly 40,500 tokens a compacted resume started from; its own cost, one pass over the context plus a summary written at the output rate and a partly uncached first call, is unreported; the saving grows with the context and with how many calls the next round makes; and compacting well below 272,000 tokens also adds headroom against the long-prompt surcharge reported above, if it applies, though whether a round stays below it depends on how much that round reads: one recorded thread resumed at 263,681 tokens and reached 282,095 within the round. Both trials compacted threads of 140,756 and 153,576 tokens, below 200,000; whether either compaction saved anything is not established, as the paragraphs before note.

The break-even behind the 150,000 recommendation is an estimate on unverified assumptions, at Codex's published Astra rates. Each call of the next round reads about the context less 40,500 tokens of cached input fewer, saving 25 credits per million of them. The compaction is assumed to cost a pass over the context, mostly cached, at 25 credits per million, a summary of about 7,600 tokens, the size of the threads' reported `last` totals after compaction, at the output rate, about 9.5 credits, and a first resumed call about 27,000 tokens less cached than it would otherwise be, about 6 credits more: roughly 15.5 credits plus the context at 25 per million. On those assumptions compaction breaks even at a context of about 173,000 tokens for a next round of 6 calls, about 135,000 for 8, about 115,000 for 10 and about 85,000 for 16, the range of 6 to 16 calls the verification rounds here made, and the two trials came out about 12 credits ahead (153,576 tokens, 11 calls) and about even (140,756 tokens, 8 calls). 150,000 therefore leaves a margin for the unknown cost and for what a summary loses only when the next round makes about 8 calls or more; before a short round, such as the 6-call one here, compacting at 150,000 would on the same assumptions lose about 3 credits. A finer rule would compact when the expected calls times the context above 40,500 tokens clearly exceed about 620,000 tokens plus the context, the estimated overhead counted as cached input.

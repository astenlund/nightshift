# Complete the spec-review safeguard and authoring guidance

## Origin

Found on 2026-09-29 by the audit of the v3 migration accounting that the user asked for, in their words: "i had initially approved cutting features for the initial v3 migration, but i didn't mean "silently remove functionality and mark it as delivered" -- what wasn't shipped should have remained/appeared in the backlog. we should look for more stuff that's unaccounted for". Independent auditors and verifiers found four retained needs that [MIGRATION_STATUS.md](../MIGRATION_STATUS.md) recorded as Policy present, whose agreed dispositions a run carries only in part. Drafted overnight for the user's triage.

## Agreed dispositions

The v3 migration agreed these on 2026-09-06, in [V3-MIGRATION.md](../../V3-MIGRATION.md):

- Gate spec-review fixes by artifact layer before editing, under Review scale and spec discipline: "Require a reviewer to explain why a detail belongs in the spec by identifying a consequential gap, contradiction, or defect in its commitments or chosen approach."
- Calibrate revise-spec against implementation-detail escalation, in the same section: "Fold the shared safeguard into the four agreed spec lenses and common review instructions, preserving contractual detail and important reasoning without demanding a complete internal implementation."
- Pre-hardening verify-fix loop, under Lifecycle and authoring proposals: "Ordinary v3 spec review challenges the overall approach, unnecessary complexity, and missing commitments so fundamental design problems are caught early."
- Authoring guidance overlay, in the same section: "Use concise Nightshift authoring guidance to capture meaningful behavior, constraints, tradeoffs, and acceptance evidence while delegating routine engineering choices."

## Current behavior

Checked on 2026-09-29 against what a run loads:

- The reviewer prompt that `internal/runtime/review.js` sends for every assessment says "do not manufacture findings or prescribe unnecessary implementation detail", and its spec lenses ask for commitments and reasoning "without contradictions, missing decisions or unnecessary prescription". [The operating brief](../../internal/workflow.md#spec-dimensions) and [revise-spec](../../skills/revise-spec/SKILL.md) ("Do not demand an unchosen implementation in prose or pseudocode") carry the same bar. Each finding carries a consequence and evidence.
- Nothing asks a spec finding to justify a demanded detail by naming the consequential gap, contradiction or defect in the spec's commitments or approach; the bar is only negative.
- The soundness lens covers feasibility ("Can the approach satisfy the commitments in the actual project and operating environment"). Nothing asks whether a simpler or different approach would better meet the commitments, or directs a challenge to unnecessary complexity.
- The brief's authoring clause asks for "a concise spec of consequential commitments, boundaries and acceptance evidence" and does not name tradeoffs; the clarity lens covers important reasoning and missing decisions only indirectly. `WORKFLOW.md` and [the v3 feature](nightshift-v3.md) ask for reasons behind important tradeoffs, but a run does not load them. This gap is the least material of the four.
- Carried: skeptic validation of every finding, whole-spec reassessment of a revised spec, review for missing commitments, delegation of routine engineering choices, and no routine plan stage.

## Direction

Add the missing parts where a run loads them, keeping the agreed stance that review criteria find missing commitments without becoming a checklist that expands every spec.

### Repair-time authoring guidance (proposed 2026-10-03)

A maintainer report of 2026-10-02 handed over twelve design-spec rules from the user's global instruction file, after the user decided that file should not prescribe Nightshift behavior and that the plugin should carry what is worth keeping. They are the bullets opening "When writing design specs, inline principle text", "In design specs, partial enumeration", "In design specs, when introducing a conditional mechanism", "In design specs, any edit, including a review fix", "In design specs, when introducing a structured field", "In design specs, every slot in a literal grammar", "In design specs, every model-owned behavior branch", "In design specs, a reciprocal pointer", "In implementation plans and design specs, when embedded code cites", "In implementation plans and design specs, when two passages assert the same thing", "In implementation plans and design specs, before citing existing test coverage" and "When trimming design spec content"; other bullets in that file touching specs, plans or designs were outside the handoff and are not settled here. The twelve rules' verbatim text is in [revision `271bd85` of the global instructions gist](https://gist.github.com/astenlund/427b43883661b68e4963b05c91b896fb/271bd859ed81cca8954880ca5b3c65febb94443d). At its triage the user observed that "probably all of these came about as a result of review fixes that were too narrow, never the initial implementation or its initial review", and asked whether they should apply to re-reviews only, all or nothing, being unsure that the rules applying in a given situation could be selected reliably. The controller proposed applying the repair-shaped rules to repairs only, distilled into one principle and attached where the runtime separates repairs from first reviews. The user accepted that as the basis for triage and left each rule's disposition to the controller's judgment, under which the details below were settled. The commitments are not yet agreed.

- One principle, applied to repairs: a repair is complete when it carries through everything it touches, and contained when it removes nothing the artifact needs. A few rules stay as calibration of what "touches" means; the other repair-shaped rules are not restated.
- No per-situation selection is needed: the whole guidance goes to each repair it covers, each calibration example names its own trigger so that on a small delta the inapplicable ones stay idle, and the runtime already tells a repair apart from a first review. The skeptic's repair proposal is where a narrow fix is designed (prevention), and the continued lead's brief (`continuationBrief` in `internal/runtime/review.js`) is where it is checked (detection). Scoping the guidance to the repair delta needs a delta the runtime does not yet supply: a continued lead now receives the cumulative change and its findings pending closure, and assesses the whole artifact. Applying the guidance to repairs rather than whole specs avoids the checklist expansion this entry guards against.
- Build on the existing seeds rather than duplicate them: the common reviewer prompt's "Prior fixes are included in the cumulative change and need surrounding/sibling coverage", the brief's reassessment "including sibling paths and previous repairs", and its signal of three successive related passes.

Dispositions of the twelve rules, by substance:

- Calibration examples of the principle, at most these: a conditional mechanism or judgment boundary states its complementary branch and its fallback when the primary path cannot run, and where no user is available to resolve the boundary (an unattended run, a denied or automatically denied prompt) it skips with a recorded note or fails closed with the structural error rather than leaving the branch undefined; stateful machinery that any edit, review fixes included, introduces or alters has its complete lifecycle specified at the governing site (creation, initialization, refresh, invalidation, scope, failure behavior, bounds, and how every consumer handles absent or stale state); two passages asserting the same thing, whether prose stating an invariant about embedded code or two statements of one value, baseline or convention, are reconciled in the same edit by deciding which is authoritative and changing or deleting the other, so a superseding statement retires what it supersedes; a structured field introduced beside an existing one mirrors the sibling's documented grammar point by point (separator, wrap-join, empty form, illegal forms, per-level placement, every lifecycle transition), marking each point the same or different, and each slot of a literal grammar is bound in the sentence that introduces the grammar (what fills it, how it is normalized, what the empty case renders).
- Covered by the principle without an example: an enumeration reads as complete, so it is completed or marks what it leaves out; a change to a value set that other parts enumerate or validate against lists every consumer and states each one's handling, the untouched ones included; a write or state described as "exactly like" an existing site gives that site's full field list or cites it; and a cross-reference or sibling update that a change promises is written in the same change, never left as a present-tense claim about text that does not yet exist. Documentation review's proportionality lens already rejects describing something "as current before it exists"; the spec lenses carry no such clause.
- The principle's contained half: a trim made on a reviewer's suggestion prefers cutting verbose justifications and implementation-tuning numbers over design reasoning, since reasoning re-derived later can miss nuances established through deliberate iteration. It meets the tradeoff gap above.
- First draft, one clause in the spec-authoring guidance: a spec states the principles it relies on instead of pointing at context local to one machine, user or session, such as memory files, scratch notes or the conversation, so it stays self-contained across sessions and machines. Review does not reliably catch this, since only a reviewer without access to that context finds the pointer unresolvable.
- Dropped as covered: verifying the exact surface of a cited framework or library API (owning package, obsoletion status, overload and member shapes) against the installed package, or against its published documentation with the citation marked provisional when it is not installed, since the soundness lens asks about "real dependencies and assumptions" and embedded code in written plans, the rule's main target, is exceptional in v3; and locating the covering assertion and naming its test file and case before citing existing coverage as the reason to omit a fixture or step, an uncited claim counting as no coverage, a plan-era rule whose main target, written plans, is exceptional in v3, and where a review finding claims coverage is missing, the skeptic validates that claim against concrete evidence before any disposition.
- Routed to [live-boundary verification](live-boundary-verification.md#v2-live-claim-markers-carried-2026-10-01): a recorded live probe covers each distinct context or marks the rest provisional, and each model-owned branch with its own marker, grammar or lifecycle exception gets its own per-host live claim rather than riding on a representative probe.

### Authoring habits added at the section-level audit's triage

[The section-level audit of v2 records](../reports/v2-section-audit-20261004.md) found three spec-authoring habits that left v2 without a disposition naming them, and the user chose on 2026-10-04 to add them to this entry's spec-authoring guidance:

- Restated text checked against its source. A spec that restates another governing document checks each restated sentence against the clause it restates before review. The retired v2 draft [Wave round economy](../migration/v2/features/wave-round-economy.md) recorded four separate review rounds that each caught one restatement that had drifted from its source, which one check before the first round would have caught together.
- One contract across siblings. When a spec defines sibling operations or surfaces, its author applies the same contract to each of them in one pass. The same draft recorded about fifteen rounds that derived such a contract one function per round.
- Why review-added machinery exists. Machinery that review adds to a spec records, in the spec, the agreed requirement that justifies it, so a later reader can tell essential mechanism from review residue and simplify safely. v2's [Contract-calibrated revise admission](contract-calibrated-revise-admission.md) proposed this as a required design-provenance section and ruled out "Reviewer requested it" as a basis. v3 enforces that ruling in each finding's disposition basis, but the run database that holds it is not part of the spec. This sits beside the tradeoff clause above.

The first two habits also fed the v2 [Authoring guidance overlay](../migration/v2/features/authoring-guidance-overlay.md), which the migration replaced with this entry's guidance without naming them.

## Open questions

- Where each clause belongs: the lens briefs and common prompt the runtime sends, the brief the controller reads, revise-spec, or several of them, and how to keep them in step.
- How a challenge to complexity and to the overall approach stays proportionate, so spec review does not reopen settled design on every pass.
- Which installed-host evidence the changed model-owned review behavior needs.
- Whether the fresh lead that follows repairs also receives the repair-time principle, scoped to the cumulative repair delta: otherwise a narrow repair the continued reviewer missed meets only the standard lenses, although that pass exists because continued reviewers develop tunnel vision. The runtime already knows when that pass is due (`reviewProgress` in `internal/runtime/lifecycle.js` reports a fresh assessment after a continued one, and each repair batch is recorded), but the prompt builder sees only whether a dispatch continues.
- Whether a reviewer's input after a repair includes the fixer's note on likely consequences and affected areas. The retirement of v2's [Fixer-triggered cell reactivation](fixer-triggered-cell-reactivation.md) kept that "Fixers can explain likely consequences and affected areas; those hints inform the independent reviewer without determining coverage" ([Repair coverage and progress](../../V3-MIGRATION.md#repair-coverage-and-progress)), but the `repair` operation records only the finding ids it repairs, so nothing delivers such a note. The user chose on 2026-10-04, at the section-level audit's triage, to settle it together with the repair delta above.
- Whether the repair-time principle applies to every review kind: the continuation brief is shared by code, documentation and spec reviews, and narrow repairs are not specific to specs.

Two entries already propose repair-time guidance for the operating brief: [Have revise-code trace the readers of state a repair changed](../QUICK_WINS.md#have-revise-code-trace-the-readers-of-state-a-repair-changed) adds a revise-code and operating-brief clause on tracing every reader and writer of state a repair changed, which overlaps the lifecycle and value-set rules above, and [Repairs start from current contents and respect helper ownership](repair-current-contents.md) has the operating brief tell repairs to keep earlier repairs' constraints, close to the principle's contained half. [Prefer the smallest repair that meets the obligation](../QUICK_WINS.md#prefer-the-smallest-repair-that-meets-the-obligation), restored at the 2026-10-04 audit's triage, has the operating brief prefer removing or generalizing machinery over adding it in every repair, the counterpart of the contained half. If this principle also reaches the operating brief, coordinate the wording so it does not gain overlapping repair statements; the controller, which applies repairs and reads the operating brief, is a third place such guidance can attach.

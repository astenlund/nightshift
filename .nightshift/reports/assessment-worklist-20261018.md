# Nightshift Assessment Worklist

Oct 18, 2026 · @Andreas Stenlund

Working checklist from the [Nightshift Assessment](assessment-20261018.md) of 2026-10-18 at commit 76c8654, plugin 3.3.12: the actions it recommends, tickable and editable here, with the reasoning, figures and the second reviewer's checks left on the page.

Terms: a fresh gate is the review a task passes only on a reviewer in a fresh context; resumed closure is the reviewer that raised a finding being resumed to confirm its repair; a closing record is the run-level review of tracking edits made after triage; a minor-only cycle is a repair batch whose findings were all minor, with the resumed closure and fresh gate the rules then require. Third, fourth and fifth reviewers checked this list against the page and the repository on 2026-10-18 and 19; their corrections are applied here. A sixth and a seventh resequenced the steps on 2026-10-20 at 3.3.13 in the maintainer's checkout, as the page records under "Independent checks"; the Latest column below carries that day's readings.

## Resumed reviewers

The backlog records that own this ground are "Make resumed reviewers actually cheap" and "Show review progress without being asked" (the per-attempt cache categories); "Adversarial repair dialogue" is a v2 record whose reviewer-to-skeptic dialogue shipped in 3.3.0, and the implementer-to-reviewer turn below has no entry of its own unless your second record covers it. These points go into those records, not new entries. The resume economy depends on prompt-cache hits, and the resume-cost record shows the misses are not timing-driven: Claude already writes to the one-hour cache and resumes minutes later read nothing, while a direct resume from another directory without a dispatch schema hit; the figures are on the page. On a miss a resume is not reliably cheaper than a fresh review.

## Fortnightly check

Eight numbers every two weeks, a pruning pass on the fortnight's entries, and a full assessment only at a milestone or when the numbers move the wrong way. Numbers 1 to 4, 7 and 8 come from the run store and receipts under each project's `.nightshift/runs`; 5 and 6 from the checkout. The readings of your three goals: 8 for fewer review-fix defects, 5 for a backlog that stops growing, 7 for features built faster. Type the latest values into the table; add a column per check.

Pruning pass, every two weeks:

- [ ] Intake first: the fold rule, filed in the index preambles after the assessed commit and shipped as "Fold fixes into the feature that replaces their code" (a fix goes into the feature that would remove or rewrite the lines it touches, not a new entry), is the admission test for every new entry; an entry that reached an index without passing it is the first thing the pass retires.
- [ ] For each entry changed in the last two weeks, ask VISION.md's four questions: what user outcome it serves, which observed failure it prevents, whether that failure still exists, whether its value warrants the complexity and recurring cost. Outputs: keep, merge or retire. Never a new entry.
- [ ] Ask one question of the documents: which sentence in VISION.md or WORKFLOW.md did this fortnight's evidence contradict? Propose one sentence change. The first such question is already known: whether instruction changes keep a prose review loop or are tested by the eval instead.
- [ ] Stop condition: if the active count has not fallen after two checks, the pass is decorating the backlog rather than pruning it.
- [ ] First pass only, bigger than the rest: apply the fold rule retroactively over the current quick wins and bugs, and resolve the roughly 29 relation sentences in the indexes ("coordinate with", "whichever ships first", "relates to") into a Requires line, a merge, a Related line with its shared-ownership sentence on both entries, or nothing; Related lines are unchecked until that feature ships, so ship it first or accept a sweep later.

## Keep

These are the product. Everything else earns its place against the detection numbers.

- The three-role review: reviewer, fresh skeptic, and a gate only a fresh reviewer can pass.
- Receipts bound to input hashes, with completion refused on stale evidence.
- SQLite state restored through hooks across compaction and closed sessions.
- Read-only reviewer copies, with probes as untrusted proposals the controller executes.
- The manifest-verified bundle copy that keeps a run's code alive across a plugin update.
- The deterministic test corpus, 19,600 lines on Windows CI and, with inspection stubbed, on Linux, and the release gate.
- The Opus and Sol cost defaults and the cap below max reasoning effort.
- The evidence discipline: every changelog entry separates deterministic evidence from model-owned behavior, and the resume-cost record is already the per-host measurement for that question.
